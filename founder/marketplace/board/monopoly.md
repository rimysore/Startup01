# Monopoly lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market it could own.** Kannada food creators plus owner-run Bengaluru cafés is narrow enough to reach by hand, and nobody in the brief owns it. Influish is national, charges creators, and its reviews say paying creators got no work.
- **The scam check answers the cold start.** Creators get scam "collab" DMs anyway. A free offer-and-sender check is useful to a single creator with zero businesses on Mitra, so the creator side can fill before the demand side does.
- **Two assets that could grow into network effects:** scam reports (shown only when 2+ creators agree, plus senders who have paid creators through Mitra), and outcome data from promo codes and counter taps (customers and cost per customer for each post). Both get better with every user and are hard to copy without the users. Neither exists yet.
- **A fair model that could become a brand.** Creators pay nothing, every fee is itemised, and the business pays only on matched deals. That is a clear contrast with the complaints about Influish.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Both sides skip the fee.** Payment already runs directly over UPI or cash. After one good deal, the café and the creator can do the next one on WhatsApp. The 10% fee only holds if Mitra keeps adding something on repeat deals: counted results, payment protection, invoices, tax paperwork. Payment protection is unbuilt and waiting on a CA. If repeat deals leave, there is no network and no revenue.
2. **No proven way to reach businesses.** Creators can arrive through the free check. Businesses arrive only when a founder walks in, and the outreach founder is part-time. Open questions: the cost to sign up a business, what Bengaluru businesses pay creators today, how much is barter, and how many deals a business runs a month. If one sign-up costs more than a few deals earn (about ₹388 net each), the model fails at any size.
3. **The copying risk is real and the edge is thin.** Offer parsing, domain checks, matching and promo codes are not hard to build. Influish has 1M+ downloads and fresh money. Instagram could add scam warnings to DMs. A local agency already knows the cafés. The only defence is getting dense in one area, with data piled up before anyone notices, and Mitra has zero deals so far.

Also on the score: this is an incremental improvement, not ten times better, and the brief states neither a "why now" nor a secret.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. What stops a café and a creator doing their second deal without Mitra, and how would you see it happening?
2. How many hours and rupees did each of your first 10 businesses cost to sign up?
3. What do Bengaluru food businesses pay creators today, and how much is barter?
4. How many creators used the scam check without being asked, and came back to it again?
5. If Influish shipped a free Kannada scam checker next month, what would you still have?
6. What do you know about this market that Influish and the agencies do not?
7. What number of cafés, creators and deals in which neighbourhoods makes Mitra the default?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Repeat deals stay on Mitra.** 3+ businesses run a second deal through Mitra with outcomes counted, and you track creator-business pairs that go quiet.
2. **The cost to sign up a business is measured** for the first 30, and it is below what one business earns Mitra over a few months.
3. **The scam check pulls creators in by itself.** A real share of the pilot creators arrive through it or through referrals, not founder DMs, and use it again unprompted.
4. **Own one dense area first.** One or two neighbourhoods and food only, with most local creator deals there going through Mitra, before adding cities, categories or the brand/agency side.
5. **Write the secret and the moat in one sentence each,** and name the data that would prove each one wrong.
