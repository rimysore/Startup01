# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The offer check makes a good front door for creators.** It is useful from minute one, with no channel access, no fee and no other side needed: forward a DM and get "likely scam / be careful / looks OK" with sources and a reply in Kannada. It removes the round 1 fear (giving an unknown app channel access) and fits a habit creators already have.
- **Care in the parts nobody sees.** A "registration fee" is not read as pay. Numbers are labelled "Self-reported" until verified. Creator reports only show when 2+ agree. Contacts are shared only on accept. Every rupee is itemised.
- **The pitch works on the creator side:** "Forward any brand offer and know in seconds if it is a scam; get paid local deals, free, and keep 100%." For the business it is: "Give Mitra your budget, get 3-5 local creators who fit, and see how many customers each one brought in." That second sentence is only true if the counting is.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** There are four products here: an analytics beta (Video Doctor, studio, trends, reports), an offer inbox, a two-sided marketplace and a WhatsApp bot. On top of that come six media-kit platforms, four ways to bring offers in, ratings, fair-rate ranges and budget splits, all before one deal has closed. Two founders, one of them part-time, cannot make four things brilliant. The analytics beta is the round 1 idea that scored 0 of 20 and should be cut. WhatsApp should stay only as a way to forward offers in.
2. **The result is measured where Mitra cannot see it.** The café comes back for "cost per customer", and that depends on someone tapping +1 at the till during a rush every time a customer says "RUCHI-SR". Payment goes over UPI or cash outside Mitra and is typed in by hand. The conversation moves to WhatsApp at the hand-off. So the talking, the paying and the counting all happen outside the product. If the till taps get missed, the report says 2 customers instead of 20, and the café decides creators don't work.
3. **The business side has no first five minutes yet.** The creator's moment is clear: a DM arrives, they forward it, they get a verdict. The café owner's is an eight-field brief, then a wait, a pick from 3-5 names, a chat, a payment and days of counting. Today they just DM a local food page. Nobody has watched an owner go through it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing? If the marketplace waited six months, would the inbox alone bring creators back every week? How many real offers does a 2K-200K Bengaluru creator get a week? (Open question: not measured.)
- Walk me through a café owner's first five minutes, on a phone, in Kannada. Who at the café taps +1, and when?
- What share of promo-code customers actually get counted at the till? (Open question: test it against the café's own bill count.)
- After the WhatsApp hand-off, how does the deal get back into Mitra, and what happens to the result if it doesn't?
- How does a checked offer lead a creator into a paid local match?
- What did you delete this month?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **One front door per side for the pilot:** the offer inbox for creators, and brief-to-match for businesses. Freeze the analytics beta and the studio and take them off the first screen.
2. **Close the counting seam before launch.** Counting should take one action per customer and be checkable, for example a code the cashier types into the bill or a link that gets redeemed. Check the counts against sales at 3+ businesses.
3. **Watch 5 café owners and 5 creators** go through their first session in person; note every stop or jump to WhatsApp before adding features.
4. **Every pilot deal goes through the payment links** (weeks 5-6), so the money and the record stay in the product.
