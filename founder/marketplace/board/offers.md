# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pay model now matches who has money.** Creators have the pain but no budget; businesses have a budget and want customers. "Creators free, business pays" follows what the round 2 panel asked for ("pay me before I pay Mitra") and avoids the "paid ₹499, got no collaborations" complaint in Influish's reviews.
- **Creators get value before there is any marketplace.** The offer check (scam patterns, missing terms, sender checks, reply drafts) solves a problem creators hit every week, costs little to run and needs no businesses on the platform. That is a real bonus in the stack, and it fills the creator side cheaply.
- **The business offer is about results, not access.** Promo codes, tracked links and cost per customer give the café owner something an Instagram ad or a cold creator DM does not: a number. The lens calls this the dream outcome: "more customers I can count", not "a post".
- **The fee is small and itemised.** ₹590 on top of a ₹5,000 deal is unlikely to be what stops anyone, and the no-dark-patterns rules support trust.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing yet shows likelihood.** On the value equation, the business side's dream outcome is strong and time is short (days), but likelihood is unproven: 0 deals, 0 measured outcomes. Nobody knows if a Kannada food creator's post brings a café 5 customers or 50. If the first café counts three walk-ins, the offer falls apart, and word spreads fast among owners on the same street. Effort also costs more than it looks: writing a brief, chatting with creators, tapping +1 at the counter during a lunch rush.
2. **The fee is easy to skip.** The money goes directly between the two sides (UPI/cash), and Mitra only records it. After one good match the café has the creator's number. A 10% fee "on top" for an introduction is a fee for access, and access is the thing you can get round. The ₹999 plan and the 5% rate do not fix this. Mitra has to keep delivering value every time (new creators, the tracking, protection, a guarantee) or it gets cut out.
3. **The money may be too thin to pay for finding businesses.** About ₹388 kept per ₹5,000 deal. Key numbers are open questions: what Bengaluru businesses pay creators today, how many deals are barter, deals per business per month, cost to win a business by walking in, repeat rate. If barter is the norm, a percentage of the creator fee comes to zero. One part-time founder signing businesses up by hand, at ₹388 a deal, may never pay back.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Of the first 10 cafés you talk to, how many have paid a creator in cash in the last 3 months, and how much?
- What result would make an owner run a second campaign, and how many customers per ₹5,000 do you expect? On what evidence?
- After the first match, what stops the café from calling the creator directly next time?
- Will owners really tap the counter, or do you need the creator's code redeemed at billing instead?
- What does winning one business cost you in hours, and how many deals does it take to earn that back?
- Could you charge double (20%, or a flat ₹1,500 a campaign) if you guaranteed a result? Would owners still choose you?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Turn it into an offer a café owner would feel stupid refusing.** For example: "Tell us your goal. We bring 3 checked local creators, write the brief, handle the chat and track every customer. You pay Mitra nothing unless the posts bring in at least X counted customers. If they don't, the next creator's Mitra fee is free." Mitra's fee is small, so this guarantee is cheap to honour. Set X only after the pilot measures it. Do it for them, so all the owner does is approve and pay.
2. **Get proof before setting the price.** By week 8: 5+ paid (not barter) deals, measured customers on 3+, and 3+ businesses coming back without being chased. Publish real case studies with real numbers ("₹5,000 → 41 customers").
3. **Find out where the fee gets skipped.** Track how often repeat deals go round Mitra. If it is common, charge for what keeps working (tracking, a supply of new creators, payment protection, the guarantee) rather than for the introduction.
4. **Answer the open questions above with real numbers** (what businesses pay creators, how many deals are barter, cost to win a business, repeat rate) before any fundraising deck uses the founders' revenue estimates.
