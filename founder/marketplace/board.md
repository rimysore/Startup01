# Board summary: Mitra marketplace, as built (round 3, 21 Oct 2026)

**Vote: 3 FUND IF. Average score 5.0 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 5, Product 5).
Round 1 (₹99 analytics coach): 3.7. Round 2 (the vision on paper): 5.7. The lenses score this lower than the vision because it is now judged on what exists: a well-built product with **0 deals, 0 measured outcomes and 0 businesses signed**.

Memos: `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md`, `board/product.md`. Inputs used for the summary: the competitor map (`competitors.md`) and two 20-buyer simulated panels (`creators/panel/results.md`, `businesses/panel/results.md`).

## Risks more than one member raised (most dangerous first)
1. **The fee gets skipped.** All three. Payment runs directly over UPI/cash and contacts are shared on accept, so after one good deal the café and the creator can do the next one on WhatsApp. A fee for the introduction is a fee for access, and access is easy to route around. Mitra has to keep adding value on every repeat deal: counting, protection, new creators.
2. **The result is measured where Mitra can't see it.** Product and Offers. Cost per customer depends on staff tapping +1 at the till during a rush. Both panels raised this independently: 9 of 20 business owners said their staff would forget, and that the count would then be wrong.
3. **No proven way to reach businesses, and thin money per deal.** Monopoly and Offers. Every business arrives by a founder walking in, and the outreach founder is part-time. Mitra keeps about ₹388 per ₹5,000 deal (₹332 after estimated guarantee costs). Still unmeasured: what it costs to sign up a business, what share of deals are barter, deals per business per month, and the repeat rate.
4. **Too many products.** Product, echoed by Monopoly. An analytics beta, an inbox, a marketplace and a WhatsApp bot, built by two founders before the first deal.

## What the panels added (simulated buyers, so treat buy rates as an upper bound)
- **Creators: 10 of 20 would sign up** (free). Small and growing creators: 56–57%. Established creators: 25%. Phone-only creators: 20%. The doubt almost all of them share: *"Will local businesses really pay cash, or just offer barter again?"* What flips a no: **seeing a creator they know get a real paid invite through Mitra**, a check that works **inside WhatsApp** without installing anything, and getting **paid on time** (one asked for the money to be held and released when the post goes live). Three suspected that "the business pays Mitra on top" just means the business offers them less.
- **Businesses: 4 of 20 would try it**, all during the free pilot. Cafés: 1 of 9. Every barter-only, ad-buying, agency and word-of-mouth owner passed. The ones who said yes had either been burned by fake influencers (2 of 3) or already pay creators in cash (2 of 4). Reasons they passed: habit 9, trust 5, convenience 2. The main point: **most local businesses pay creators in free meals, not cash**, and Mitra's offer assumes cash. What flips them: **free code tracking for creators they find themselves or pay in barter**, **counting that doesn't rely on staff**, **proof from a similar business nearby**, Verified-only creators, and **paying per customer who actually comes in**.
- **Fee price** (Mitra's fee on a ₹5,000 deal, 19 answers): bargain ₹199, getting expensive ₹400, too expensive ₹750. ₹500 is inside the acceptable range (₹100–600) but above the median "expensive" answer.
- **Creator Pro** (optional, later): acceptable range ₹50–300 a month, median bargain ₹79.

## The competition, in one paragraph (`competitors.md`)
The category is crowded and young: CollabVue, Hypee, Localyse, Restronaut and Crafted all launched in 2025–26 and none shows traction. Influish (1.7M installs, 3.3★) charges creators and gets "paid, got no collabs" reviews. Instagram's creator marketplace is free. On a ₹5,000 deal, businesses pay an intermediary anything from ₹0 (Hypee, Localyse, Instagram, DMs) through ₹500 (Restronaut's 10%) to about ₹2,500 per creator at agencies, which also have ₹50k–1 lakh minimums. Every scam checker the researcher found is free and in English. **The gap:** nobody combines neighbourhood-level, Kannada matching with a per-deal fee a small business can afford, customers counted at the counter, and free, India-aware scam checks for creators who never pay. The gap is narrow on price, since free tools exist and the fee has to be earned through results. **The closest threat is Crafted**, which is Bengaluru-only, restaurant-focused and still working out its pricing.

## Conditions (merged checklist)
- [ ] **One front door per side.** Creators get the offer inbox; businesses get brief-to-match. Freeze the analytics beta and the studio, and take them off the first screen. (Product, Monopoly)
- [ ] **Fix the counting seam before launch.** Counting should take one action per customer and be checkable against sales at 3+ businesses. (Product, Offers; 9 of 20 businesses)
- [ ] **Make the offer impossible to refuse.** Do the work for the owner and attach a guarantee: if no customers are counted, the next match is free. (Offers)
- [ ] **Accept barter.** Let businesses track barter deals and creators they find themselves, free. Use the counted results to move them from barter to paid. (panel)
- [ ] **Payment links for every pilot deal**, with the creator's fee held and released when the post goes live, once a CA signs off. (Product, Monopoly; creator panel)
- [ ] **Proof by week 8:** 5+ paid (not barter) deals, customers counted on 3+, and 3+ businesses coming back unprompted. Publish real case studies. (all three)
- [ ] **Track whether the fee gets skipped.** Count creator–business pairs that go quiet after one deal. Keep 3+ repeat deals on Mitra. (Monopoly, Offers)
- [ ] **Measure what it costs to sign up a business** for the first 30, and keep it below what a business earns Mitra over a few months. (Monopoly)
- [ ] **The scam check brings creators in by itself:** a real share arrive through it or by referral, and use it again without being asked. (Monopoly)
- [ ] **One dense area first:** 1–2 neighbourhoods, food only. (Monopoly)
- [ ] **Answer the open numbers** (what businesses pay creators today, barter share, deals per business per month, repeat rate) before any deck uses the revenue estimates. (Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] **Watch 5 owners and 5 creators** use it for the first time, in person. (Product)
- [ ] **Write the secret and the moat in one sentence each**, and name the data that would prove each wrong. (Monopoly)

## Where they disagree
- **Offers** would hold the price and add a guarantee; it thinks the fee is too small to stop anyone and suggests it could even double with a guarantee. The **business panel** disagrees: ₹500 is already past the median "getting expensive" point, and the real objection is paying cash at all.
- **Monopoly** thinks the scam check is the real moat (reports from creators build a network effect). **Product** thinks it is the front door, but doubts creators get enough offers a week to return weekly; that number is unmeasured.
- **Product** wants the analytics beta cut. The round 2 plan wanted it kept as the "growth core" for connected creators. Freezing it, rather than deleting it, satisfies both.

## The strongest version the board can see
Not "a marketplace where businesses pay 10% for introductions". It is **a free customer counter that Bengaluru food businesses use for every creator post, including barter and creators they found themselves**, plus a free scam-and-terms check for creators. Mitra charges only when it brings a creator who beats the business's own picks on counted customers, with a guarantee if it doesn't. The counted outcomes are the asset: they turn barter into cash deals ("your free-meal creator brought 4 customers; this ₹3,000 creator brought 31"), they are what keeps repeat deals on Mitra, and nobody in the competitor map has them. This differs from what is built in three ways. Tracking becomes free and open to deals Mitra didn't bring. Barter becomes a supported deal type. Counting moves from staff tapping +1 to a customer-side coupon. The money model changes with it; see `GUIDE.md`.
