## 3. FEMA: founders across two countries

### Option A: Indian Pvt Ltd, with the US-resident founder as a shareholder
- Shares held by a person resident outside India are **FDI** under the FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules 2019. Subscribing to the MoA at incorporation is at face value. Later issues must be at or above fair value certified by a CA or merchant banker. **File FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment**, and an **FLA return by 15 July** each year ([RBI Master Direction, Foreign Investment](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=11200); [ICSI FC-GPR checklist](https://www.icsi.edu/media/filer_public/c9/88/c9886f27-4539-4ff7-abc8-d06de6d141aa/checklist_for_filing_of_form_fc-gpr.pdf); [RBI FLA FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/FEMA1999FLA14112022_EN.pdf)).
- **If the US-resident founder is an NRI or OCI**, investing on a **non-repatriation basis** is treated as domestic investment, which is simpler ([RBI MD, Annex 4](https://avantiscdnprodstorage.blob.core.windows.net/legalupdatedocs/38674/RBI-issued-an-update-in-the-Master-Direction-Foreign-Investment-in-India-JAN172025.pdf)). Whether this applies depends on the founder's citizenship and residential status. **The lawyer must confirm it.**
- **Sector:** e-commerce in services is 100% automatic route for the **marketplace model**; FDI is banned in the inventory model ([PIB, 26 Dec 2018, Press Note 2](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1557380)). Mitra must never buy creator services and resell them. If the company is foreign-owned or controlled, the marketplace conditions apply: no influence on prices, and an auditor's certificate to RBI by 30 September each year. They arguably do not apply if the non-resident founder holds under 50% without control. That is an interpretation for counsel.

### Option B: Delaware parent with an Indian subsidiary (a "flip")
- **The India-resident founder's shares in the Delaware parent are ODI** under the Overseas Investment Rules 2022, within the **LRS limit of $250,000 a year** ([RBI LRS FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonperson/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1834)).
- **The big catch:** under Schedule III, a resident individual may invest only in a foreign entity that **does not have a subsidiary in which the individual has control**. Control starts at **10% of voting rights**. So an India-resident co-founder with ≥10% of a Delaware parent that owns the Indian operating company **falls outside the general permission** and needs approval or a different design, such as under 10% without control, or ESOP or sweat equity ([TaxGuru, 20 Aug 2023](https://taxguru.in/rbi/overseas-investment-rules-2022-resident-individuals-indian-entities.html); [Infinilex, 29 Sep 2026](https://infinilex.io/insights/round-tripping-two-layer-rule-fema/)). The India-based founder also files an **Annual Performance Report by 31 December**.
- **Tax on the flip:** swapping Indian shares for Delaware shares is a taxable transfer, and no Sec 47 exemption covers it ([LexVerge, 17 Sep 2026](https://lexverge.com/blog/delaware-flip-indian-startup-fema-tax/)). **Flipping while the company is worth almost nothing keeps this tax near zero**; flipping later is expensive.
- **Reversing a flip later is very expensive:** Groww paid ~₹1,340 cr, Razorpay ~₹1,245 cr and Meesho ~$288M to move back to India before listing ([Moneycontrol, 29 May 2025](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/razorpay-to-pay-rs-1-245-crore-in-taxes-to-flip-back-to-india-ipo-likely-in-18-24-months-13064580.html); [Moneycontrol, 16 Jun 2025](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/meesho-to-pay-288-million-in-taxes-to-flip-back-to-india-ahead-of-ipo-13125085.html)). ET names YC's parent-entity rule and reverse-flip taxes as reasons India's YC count fell from 66 (2021) to 4 (2024) ([ET](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/startups/y-combinators-india-cohort-shrinks-amid-local-capital-reshaping-deals-ai-focus/articleshow/122326955.cms)).

## 4. Payments: why Mitra must not hold the money

- RBI's **Master Direction on Payment Aggregators (15 Sep 2025)** defines a PA as anyone who collects payments for merchants and later settles them. A non-bank PA needs **₹15 cr net worth on applying and ₹25 cr by the end of year 3**, and must keep funds in escrow. A marketplace counts as a "merchant", and **a PA may not run a marketplace business** ([RBI MD](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896); [FE, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/banking-finance-rbi-issues-final-guidelines-for-payment-aggregators-3978902/)).
- So: **never take a business's money into Mitra's bank account and pay creators from it.** Use an authorised PA's split product. Money sits in the PA's escrow and settles directly to each creator's KYC'd account; Mitra receives only its own fee.
  - **Razorpay Route:** each creator is a Linked Account with PAN plus bank or UPI, verified by penny-drop. Transfers can be held and released, which fits "base fee on posting, bonus weekly" ([Route docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/); [schedule settlements](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/schedule-settlement)).
  - **Cashfree Easy Split:** per-order splits. **Vendors who don't complete KYC are blocked from settlement** ([Cashfree Easy Split](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/overview); [vendor KYC](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/vendor/kyc)).
- **Risk:** one research pass found Route eligibility now tied to merchant turnover checks under the 2025 rules. A pre-revenue company may not get it switched on at first. **Ask both providers in writing before building on either.** Until then, keep the current plan: payment links for Mitra's own plan fee only, and creators paid directly.

## 5. GST and TDS (Income-tax Act 2025, in force from 1 Apr 2026)

- **GST at 18%** on Mitra's plans. Registration is required above ₹20 lakh turnover in Karnataka, and voluntary registration lets you invoice businesses properly ([KGST s.22](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/KGSTAct2017notificatins%20010118_eng.pdf)).
- **If deal money flows through Mitra's split account**, Mitra is likely an **e-commerce operator collecting consideration**:
  - **Mandatory GST registration regardless of turnover** (CGST s.24(x)).
  - **TCS at 0.5%** (from 10 Jul 2024) on supplies by GST-registered creators. Creators below ₹20 lakh are exempt from registering, and no TCS applies to them.
  - Sources: [CBIC s.24](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section24_v1.00.html), [Notification 15/2024](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/central-tax-15-2024-11072024.pdf), [Notification 65/2017](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-65-central-tax-english.pdf).
  - Whether a PA-run split counts as Mitra "collecting" is **not settled in official guidance**. Ask the CA.
- **TDS:**
  - **194-O is now s.393(1) Table Sl. 8(v): 0.1%** on gross amounts by e-commerce participants. Sources disagree on whether the ₹5 lakh threshold for individuals carried over.
  - **194R** (benefits above ₹20,000 a year, for example free meals or products to a creator) is now Sl. 8(iv) at 10%, and the duty falls on the business.
  - 194J and 194C also moved into s.393.
  - Sources: [incometaxindia TDS FAQ](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/what-are-the-payments-covered-under-the-tds-mechanism-and-the-rates-for-deduction-of-tax-at-source-); [TaxGuru chart, 1 May 2026](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/complete-tds-rate-chart-fy-2026-27-section-wise-breakdown.html); [CBDT Circular 12/2022](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/circular-no-12-2022-062fe002fcd64e9-62818365.pdf).
  - **Update the docs:** `BUSINESS_MODEL.md` and `SHORT-TERM-PLAN.md` still cite the old section numbers.

## 6. Recommendation on entity structure (a CA and lawyer must confirm)

**Recommended (judgement):** **incorporate an Indian Pvt Ltd now, registered in Karnataka. Apply to YC W27 before incorporating; YC says you don't need to. Flip to a US parent only if a YC offer or a US lead investor requires it, and then do it immediately, while the company is worth close to nothing.**

Why:
1. **100% of customers, revenue and staff are in India**, and Mitra will most likely list in India if it ever lists. Recent reverse flips cost Groww, Razorpay and Meesho hundreds of millions of dollars.
2. **Every Indian benefit attaches to an Indian entity:** DPIIT, Sec 140 (80-IAC), the next ELEVATE call, Karnataka incentives, FoF-backed VCs, and Indian angels who expect DPIIT. A Delaware parent fails the 51% Indian-promoter test that SISFS used.
3. **The Delaware route has a specific FEMA problem for the India-based co-founder:** the ≥10% control rule in ODI Schedule III. It is solvable, but it needs a lawyer and may need approval. Doing it before anyone has asked for it adds cost and risk for no benefit.
4. YC accepts companies that have no entity yet and helps create the parent after acceptance, so incorporating in India first does not shut that door. It means a flip later, which is cheap while the value is tiny.

| | Indian Pvt Ltd (recommended now) | Delaware parent + Indian subsidiary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup cost and time | Lower; days to weeks | Higher: US formation, registered agent, Delaware franchise tax and US tax filings, plus the Indian subsidiary |
| Indian schemes (DPIIT, ELEVATE, Karnataka) | Eligible | Only the subsidiary; fails "Indian promoter" tests |
| YC / US VCs | Needs a flip on acceptance | Ready |
| India-based co-founder | Simple | ODI filings; ≥10% control rule may need approval; APR by 31 Dec |
| US-resident co-founder | FDI filings (FC-GPR, FLA), or simpler if NRI/OCI on a non-repatriation basis | Simple |
| Indian IPO later | Simple | Costly reverse flip |
| E-commerce FDI conditions | Apply only if foreign-owned or controlled | The Indian subsidiary is foreign-owned, so they apply (auditor certificate by 30 Sep) |

**Questions to take to the CA and lawyer this week:**
- (a) the US-resident founder's citizenship and FEMA residential status, and whether a non-repatriation NRI or OCI holding works;
- (b) the founders' split and vesting;
- (c) how to design a later flip around the ODI Schedule III 10% rule;
- (d) whether split settlement through Route or Easy Split makes Mitra an e-commerce operator for GST TCS and TDS;
- (e) GST registration from day one;
- (f) terms of service that make Mitra a platform, not a party to deals.
