# B. India schemes and rules for Mitra (researched 21 Oct 2026)

All sources were read on 21 Oct 2026. Where a page shows a date, it is given. "Read" means the page has no date. Where sources disagree, both are shown. "Not found" means the research turned up nothing.

## 1. DPIIT Startup India recognition

- **New definition.** DPIIT Notification **G.S.R. 108(E) dated 4 Feb 2026** replaces the 2019 definition. [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/dpiit-revises-startup-definition-introduces-deep-tech-framework/) (read 21 Oct 2026); [Income Tax Dept](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/income-from-business/profession-taxation-of-start-ups) (read).
- **Eligible entities:** Pvt Ltd company, registered partnership, LLP, multi-state cooperative or state cooperative society. A Delaware C-corp cannot be recognised; only its Indian subsidiary can.
- **Age:** up to 10 years from incorporation (20 years for a "Deep Tech Startup").
- **Turnover:** at most **₹200 cr** in every FY since incorporation, up from ₹100 cr (₹300 cr for deep tech).
- **Innovation test:** working on innovation or improvement of products, processes or services, **or** a scalable business model with high potential for jobs or wealth creation.
- **Exclusion:** an entity formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business does not qualify. [PIB 5 Feb 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2224069&reg=3&lang=1); [Startup India Tax Playbook, May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).
- **Deep Tech category** needs: new scientific or engineering knowledge, high R&D spend, significant novel IP, and long gestation. Mitra (a marketplace/SaaS) almost certainly does **not** qualify. [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).
- **Fund-use rules.** The 2026 framework adds conditions on how funds are used. Commentary refers to a "negative list" of non-core investments, but Taxmann did not list it, so the exact list was not found. Recognition can be revoked for misrepresentation. [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/dpiit-revises-startup-definition-introduces-deep-tech-framework/) (read 21 Oct 2026).
- **How to apply:** recognition is applied for on the National Single Window System or the Startup India portal. Recognition only makes a startup eligible to *apply* for each benefit; every benefit has its own process. [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).
- **Benefits of recognition:**
  - 80-IAC tax holiday (needs a separate IMB certificate).
  - ESOP tax deferral: Sec 192 of the 1961 Act, now Sec 392/289 of the 2025 Act.
  - Relaxed loss carry-forward: Sec 79, now Sec 119.
  - Angel-tax relief, which is now moot.
  - Access to SISFS, FoF and CGSS.
  - Source: [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).
- **Scale:** more than 55,200 startups were recognised in FY 2025-26, a record, and the total is over 2.23 lakh. [PIB 17 Apr 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=2&PRID=2253019&reg=3).

## 2. Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS)

- **Amounts:** up to **₹20 lakh grant** for proof of concept, prototype or product trials, plus up to **₹50 lakh** as convertible debentures or debt for market entry and scale-up. Money is paid in milestone-based tranches through incubators. [Startup Schemes Playbook, June 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/homebanners/Startup-Schemes-Playbook-June-2026.pdf); [Lok Sabha USQ 323, 21 Jul 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU323_AhXfm3.pdf).
- **Eligibility:**
  - DPIIT-recognised.
  - Incorporated **no more than 2 years** before applying.
  - Uses technology in its core product or business model.
  - Has received no more than ₹10 lakh from other government schemes.
  - **Shareholding by Indian promoters at least 51%** at the time of application.
  - Source: [SISFS Guidelines](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/invest-india/Templates/public/Guidelines%20for%20Startup%20India%20Seed%20Fund%20Scheme.pdf) (read 21 Oct 2026).
- **Status: CLOSED to new applicants.** The portal's final notice says the last date for startup applications was **31 May 2026**, and incubators had to finish selection by **30 Jun 2026**. Payouts to startups already selected are continuing. [seedfund.startupindia.gov.in](https://seedfund.startupindia.gov.in/) (read 21 Oct 2026); [Schemes Playbook June 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/homebanners/Startup-Schemes-Playbook-June-2026.pdf).
- **The extension only covers existing commitments.** The government says the scheme "has recently been provided an extension to facilitate disbursement of committed funds". [LS USQ 323, 21 Jul 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU323_AhXfm3.pdf); [PIB 17 Apr 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=2&PRID=2253019&reg=3).
- **No successor found.** No SISFS 2.0 or new application window was found as of 6 Oct 2026. [startupindiax, 7 Oct 2026](https://startupindiax.com/sisfs-cgss-or-fof-2-pick-the-right-scheme/) (secondary source).
- **Scheme size:** 219 incubators, the full ₹945 cr corpus committed, and 4,245 startups selected with ₹706.33 cr approved (as of 30 Jun 2026). [LS USQ 1446, 28 Jul 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1446_N4i3CT.pdf).
- **Karnataka:** 22 incubators with ₹104.48 cr, and 542 startups with ₹104.87 cr approved. [LS USQ 3746](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3746_gzMvwh.pdf); [LS USQ 1446](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1446_N4i3CT.pdf) (both July 2026).
- **Bengaluru incubators.** C-CAMP is one named SISFS incubator (13 startups, ₹4.55 cr). The full list is on the [SISFS portfolio page](https://seedfund.startupindia.gov.in/portfolio). A verified list of all Bengaluru SISFS incubators (NSRCEL, SID-IISc and others) was **not found**. This matters less now that applications are closed.
- **Effect on Mitra:**
  - Even if SISFS reopened, a **Delaware-parent structure fails the 51% Indian-promoter test**, because the Indian subsidiary's promoter is a foreign company.
  - An Indian Pvt Ltd where the US-resident co-founder holds more than 49% may also fail. Whether a non-resident Indian-citizen founder counts as an "Indian promoter" was not found; this needs advice.

## 3. Section 80-IAC tax holiday (Sec 140 of the Income-tax Act 2025)

- **Benefit:** 100% deduction of profits for **any 3 consecutive years out of the first 10** from incorporation.
- **Who qualifies:** a Pvt Ltd or LLP incorporated on or after 1 Apr 2016 and **before 1 Apr 2030**, holding an **IMB certificate**.
- **Deadline extension.** Budget 2025 extended the incorporation deadline from 1 Apr 2025 to 1 Apr 2030. [PIB 15 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2128860&lang=2&reg=3); [Startup India IMB page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/imb.html) (updated 14 Sep 2026).
- **New section number.** From 1 Apr 2026 the provision is **Sec 140 of the Income-tax Act 2025**. [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).
- **Turnover cap: sources conflict.**
  - ₹100 cr: [Income Tax Dept](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/income-from-business/profession-taxation-of-start-ups) (read) and the [Startup India 80-IAC form](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/form80iac.html) (14 Sep 2026).
  - ₹300 cr "in the relevant tax year": [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).
  - Verify against the text of Sec 140. Either way, the cap does not bind Mitra.
- **IMB process:**
  - Apply on the Startup India portal with Form-1.
  - Documents include the MoA, ITRs, audited financials, a CA certificate (not formed by splitting up an existing business), a scalability declaration (for example more than 10% year-on-year revenue growth), IP, awards, the pitch deck and employment data.
  - DPIIT says complete applications are reviewed within 120 days.
  - About 3,700 startups had been approved by May 2025, against more than 2 lakh recognised, so approval is selective.
  - Sources: [80-IAC form](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/form80iac.html); [PIB 15 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2128860&lang=2&reg=3).
- **Effect on Mitra:** the holiday only helps once Mitra makes a profit, and MAT/AMT may still apply. [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf).

## 4. Angel tax (Sec 56(2)(viib))

- **Abolished** for all classes of investors. The Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 added a proviso that the section does not apply from 1 Apr 2025 (AY 2025-26 onward). [Tax Playbook May 2026](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/startupindia/Tax-Playbook-for-Startup-Ecosystem-May-2026.pdf) ("abolished with effect from 1st April 2025"); [KPMG Aug 2024](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2024/08/abolishment-of-angel-tax-a-welcome-move.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/angel-tax-abolished-section-56-2-viib-scrutiny-lives-on-reality-check-ay-2026-27-filing-season.html) (2026).
- **One related tax remains.** Tax on *receiving* shares below fair value still applies. It was Sec 56(2)(x) and is now Sec 92(2)(m) of the 2025 Act. This matters for a share-swap flip. [LexVerge, 17 Sep 2026](https://lexverge.com/blog/delaware-flip-indian-startup-fema-tax/) (secondary).
- **FEMA pricing still applies:** shares issued to non-residents cannot be priced below fair value. See section 7 below.

## 5. Fund of Funds: FFS 1.0 and Startup India FoF 2.0

- **FFS 1.0 (2016, run by SIDBI).**
  - The full ₹10,000 cr corpus is committed to 145 AIFs.
  - As of 30 Jun 2026, the AIFs had invested ₹27,902.7 cr in 1,460 startups.
  - In Karnataka, ₹6,218.65 cr went into 295 startups over 2021–25.
  - Sources: [PIB 14 Feb 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2227988&reg=3&lang=1); [LS USQ 3746, Jul 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3746_gzMvwh.pdf).
- **FoF 2.0.**
  - Announced in Budget 2025 and approved by the Cabinet on **14 Feb 2026** with a **₹10,000 cr** corpus.
  - Priorities: deep tech, tech-driven manufacturing, early-growth startups, and reach beyond the metros.
  - Source: [PIB 14 Feb 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2227988&reg=3&lang=1).
- **How FoF 2.0 reaches startups.**
  - Operational guidelines were issued **25 Apr 2026**.
  - Money goes **only through SEBI-registered Category I and II AIFs**, which then invest in **DPIIT-recognised** startups. SIDBI is the first implementing agency.
  - AIF segments: deep tech, micro-VC for early-growth startups, manufacturing, and sector-agnostic funds. Minimum private-capital ratios apply.
  - The fund is "a catalyst rather than a direct investor".
  - Sources: [PIB 25 Apr 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2255545&reg=3&lang=1); [guidelines PDF](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/04/8949d8e0dabf11cb9291b00ec1d1f727.pdf).
  - One secondary source found no sanctioned FoF 2.0 AIFs as of 6 Oct 2026. [startupindiax](https://startupindiax.com/sisfs-cgss-or-fof-2-pick-the-right-scheme/).
- **Effect on Mitra:** there is no direct application. Mitra benefits only if an FoF-backed VC invests, and those VCs can only count investments in DPIIT-recognised (Indian) entities.
- **Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups (CGSS):** the cover per borrower rose from ₹10 cr to ₹20 cr in FY 2025-26. [PIB 17 Apr 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?lang=2&PRID=2253019&reg=3).

## 7. FEMA: FDI into an Indian Pvt Ltd, and ODI for a Delaware parent

### (a) Indian Pvt Ltd with a US-resident co-founder

- **Shares held by a person resident outside India count as FDI** under the FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules 2019.
- **Subscribing to the Memorandum of Association at incorporation is done at face value**, subject to the entry route and sector caps (RBI Master Direction para 8.5).
- **Later share issues** must be priced at or above fair value, using an internationally accepted method certified by a CA, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a cost accountant (para 8.1).
- **Share swaps** need a valuation by a SEBI-registered merchant banker or an overseas investment banker (para 8.4).
- Source: [RBI MD Foreign Investment, as updated 15 Jan 2025](https://avantiscdnprodstorage.blob.core.windows.net/legalupdatedocs/38674/RBI-issued-an-update-in-the-Master-Direction-Foreign-Investment-in-India-JAN172025.pdf); [RBI MD page](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=11200).
- **Reporting:**
  - **Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment**, filed on the FIRMS portal with the FIRC, KYC, valuation report and CS certificate.
  - Shares must be issued within 60 days of receiving the money (Companies Act), and the money must be refunded if shares are not issued within 180 days (FEMA).
  - **FLA return by 15 July every year** while FDI is outstanding.
  - Sources: [ICSI FC-GPR checklist](https://www.icsi.edu/media/filer_public/c9/88/c9886f27-4539-4ff7-abc8-d06de6d141aa/checklist_for_filing_of_form_fc-gpr.pdf); [RBI FLA FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/FEMA1999FLA14112022_EN.pdf).
- **If the US-resident founder is an NRI or OCI, there is a simpler route.** Investment on a **non-repatriation basis** (Schedule IV / Annex 4) is "deemed to be domestic investment at par with residents". The money must come through NRE/FCNR/NRO accounts or banking channels, and sale proceeds can go only to an NRO account. A US citizen who is not of Indian origin and holds no OCI card cannot use this. [RBI MD Annex 4](https://avantiscdnprodstorage.blob.core.windows.net/legalupdatedocs/38674/RBI-issued-an-update-in-the-Master-Direction-Foreign-Investment-in-India-JAN172025.pdf).
- **E-commerce FDI rules (Press Note 2 of 2018, effective 1 Feb 2019):**
  - "E-commerce" means buying and selling of goods **and services** over a digital network.
  - **100% FDI under the automatic route is allowed for the marketplace model.**
  - **FDI is banned in the inventory model.**
  - Marketplace conditions:
    - No ownership or control of inventory.
    - No direct or indirect influence on sale prices.
    - Services to vendors on arm's-length, non-discriminatory terms.
    - Seller details shown on the platform.
    - Payments facilitated only in line with RBI guidelines.
    - No mandatory exclusivity.
    - **An auditor's certificate to RBI by 30 September every year.**
  - "Sale of services through e-commerce shall be under automatic route" (subject to sector conditions).
  - Sources: [PIB 26 Dec 2018](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1557380); [DPIIT PN2(2018) PDF](https://master-dpiit.digifootprint.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/3f232440ca3fbdfd9ea2ab384d3813b9.pdf).
- **How the rules fit Mitra.** Mitra connects businesses (buyers) with creators (service sellers) and charges a SaaS fee, so it fits the marketplace or IT-platform description. It must never buy creator services itself and resell them.
- **The marketplace conditions may not bind at all.** They apply to an "e-commerce entity" that is *owned or controlled by a person resident outside India*. If the US-resident founder holds less than 50% and does not control the company (or invests as an NRI/OCI on a non-repatriation basis), Mitra arguably falls outside them. This is an interpretation that needs confirming with counsel.
- **SaaS:** pure SaaS (IT services) is also 100% automatic route. A specific citation was not fetched.
- **Press Note 3 (2020)** applies only to investors from countries sharing a land border with India, so it does not affect a US founder.
- **Budget 2026-27 (1 Feb 2026)** announced a "comprehensive review" of the FEMA NDI Rules. No outcome was found. [EY Budget 2026 startup highlights](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/services/tax/union-budget-2026/ey-start-up-sector-highlights.pdf).

### (b) Delaware parent with an Indian subsidiary

- **The India-resident founder's Delaware shares are ODI.** Any investment by a resident in the *unlisted* equity of a foreign entity counts as ODI. Individuals act under **Schedule III of the OI Rules 2022** and within the **LRS cap of USD 250,000 per FY**. [RBI LRS FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonperson/english/scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1834); [RBI A.P. Circular 2015](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Notification.aspx?Id=9756).
- **Key barrier (Schedule III para 1(2)(i)).** A resident individual may make ODI only "in an operating foreign entity not engaged in financial services activity and **which does not have subsidiary or step-down subsidiary where the resident individual has control** in the foreign entity".
  - "Control" starts at **10% of voting rights**, a board majority, or control of management.
  - So an India-resident founder with 10% or more of a Delaware HoldCo that owns the Indian OpCo **falls outside the general permission**. That needs approval through the AD bank and RBI, or a different design, such as staying under 10% without control, or using an ESOP or sweat equity, which are exempt.
  - Sources: [ELP Law](https://elplaw.in/leadership/government-revamps-overseas-investment-rules-allows-investment-in-financial-services-deferred-payment-of-consideration-other-changes/); [TaxGuru FAQ, 20 Aug 2023](https://taxguru.in/rbi/overseas-investment-rules-2022-resident-individuals-indian-entities.html); [Infinilex, 29 Sep 2026](https://infinilex.io/insights/round-tripping-two-layer-rule-fema/); [Deloitte/INMACS deck](https://aicas.in/3rd-Fema-Workshop-Mailer/pdf/Overseas-Direct-Investmnet-III-CA-Vinod-Jain.pdf).
- **Round-tripping (Rule 19(3)).** No resident may invest in a foreign entity that invests back into India if the result is **more than two layers of subsidiaries**. A simple Delaware → India structure is within that limit, but the individual-control barrier above is what bites. [SCC Online, 14 Feb 2025](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/02/14/round-tripping/).
- **ODI reporting:**
  - Form FC at the time of investment, plus a UIN.
  - An **Annual Performance Report by 31 December every year**, unless the holding is under 10% without control and there is no other financial commitment.
  - Source: [RBI OI Regulations 2022, reg 10(4)](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasterDirections.aspx?id=12380).
- **Tax on a flip (India → Delaware share swap):**
  - The swap is a "transfer" under Sec 2(47). Indian capital-gains tax is due on the FMV of the Delaware shares received minus cost: 12.5% LTCG if held more than 24 months, otherwise slab rates.
  - **No Sec 47 exemption covers a founder-level swap into a foreign company.**
  - The FEMA legs also need FC-TRS within 60 days and fair-value pricing.
  - Flipping early, at a low valuation, keeps the tax small. For example, a 30% stake in a ₹40 cr company means roughly ₹1.8 cr of tax with no cash received.
  - Sources: [LexVerge, 17 Sep 2026](https://lexverge.com/blog/delaware-flip-indian-startup-fema-tax/); [Virtual Auditor, 20 Mar 2026](https://virtualauditor.in/learn/flip-structure-india-delaware-fema/); [Sec 47 text](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-47-61).
- **Reverse flips (companies moving back to India):**
  - **PhonePe** (from Singapore, 2022-23): about ₹8,000 cr in tax.

## 8. RBI Payment Aggregator rules

- **Current rules:** the **Master Direction on Regulation of Payment Aggregators, RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141, dated 15 Sep 2025**, effective immediately. It replaces the 2020 guidelines. [RBI MD](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896).
- **Definitions:**
  - A **PA** aggregates customer payments to merchants and "subsequently settles the collected funds to such merchants".
  - A **marketplace** is an e-commerce entity providing an IT platform between buyers and sellers.
  - A **"merchant" includes a marketplace.**
  - "A PA business shall not carry out marketplace business" (para 10(b)).
  - A PA must ensure that a marketplace it onboards "does not accept payments for a seller not onboarded on to the marketplace's platform" (para 13(f)).
  - Source: [RBI MD](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896).
- **Net worth for authorisation:** **₹15 cr when applying, ₹25 cr by the end of the 3rd FY**, maintained on an ongoing basis. CCPS count toward it. Banks need no authorisation. [RBI MD para 6](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896); [FE, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/banking-finance-rbi-issues-final-guidelines-for-payment-aggregators-3978902/).
- **Escrow:**
  - A non-bank PA keeps merchant funds in an escrow account at a scheduled commercial bank (rules to be followed by 31 Dec 2025).
  - Permitted debits include payments to onboarded merchants, and payments to third parties on a merchant's instruction where the merchant's turnover is above ₹40 lakh and the third party is the payee for the underlying transaction.
  - Merchant due diligence: simplified KYC for merchants with turnover up to ₹40 lakh (PAN, contact-point verification, one officially valid document).
  - Merchants onboarded from 1 Jan 2026 must follow the full due-diligence rules.
  - Source: [RBI MD paras 13, 16](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896).
- **Effect on Mitra:**
  - Mitra cannot meet ₹15 cr of net worth. It should **never collect business money into its own account and pay creators out**, because that is PA activity.
  - Instead, use an authorised PA's split product, so money moves from the PA's escrow directly to each creator's KYC'd linked account and Mitra keeps only its commission.
  - **Razorpay Route:** "split incoming funds among multiple third-parties, sellers".
    - Each creator becomes a **Linked Account** with KYC: PAN, bank account or UPI ID, and penny-drop verification.
    - GSTIN is needed only for vendor businesses with turnover above ₹20 lakh.
    - Settlement takes 2 working days by default, or is near-instant to a UPI ID (up to ₹1 lakh per transfer).
    - Transfers can be held, which suits paying a per-customer bonus later.
    - Sources: [Route docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/); [Linked Accounts](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/linked-account); [Composite API](https://razorpay.com/docs/api/payments/route/create-linked-account-composite); [Schedule settlements](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/schedule-settlement) (all read 21 Oct 2026).
  - **Cashfree Easy Split:** split payments for marketplaces at order level.
    - Vendor KYC: individuals need PAN plus an address proof; businesses need PAN and CIN, plus GSTIN if settlements to one PAN exceed ₹20 lakh over 12 months.
    - **Vendors who do not complete KYC are blocked from settlement.**
    - Sources: [Easy Split overview](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/overview); [Vendor KYC](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/vendor/kyc) (read 21 Oct 2026).

## 9. GST and TDS

### GST

- **Platform fees and subscriptions** (the ₹499 and ₹1,499 plans) are "all other services", taxed at **18%**. [GST Council services rate booklet](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/services-booklet-03july2017.pdf). Whether the Sept 2025 GST rate changes moved these services was **not found**; no source suggests they did.
- **Influencer services** are generally taxed at 18%. **Barter (free products for content) is a taxable supply at open market value.** [APMH (secondary)](https://apmhconsulting.com/insights/APD-006_GST_Influencers); [KGST Act s.7, "barter"](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/KGSTAct2017notificatins%20010118_eng.pdf).
- **Registration threshold for services:** ₹20 lakh, or ₹10 lakh in special-category states. Karnataka is ₹20 lakh. [KGST s.22](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/KGSTAct2017notificatins%20010118_eng.pdf).
- **E-commerce operator rules:**
  - **Every ECO that must collect TCS has to register regardless of turnover** (CGST s.24(x)). [CBIC s.24](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter6/section24_v1.00.html).
  - **TCS under s.52** applies only where the ECO **collects the consideration**.
  - The rate was cut from 1% to **0.5%** (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST) from **10 Jul 2024**, by Notification 15/2024-CT. [GST Council PDF](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/central-tax-15-2024-11072024.pdf); [TaxGuru, 10 Jul 2024](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/cgst-e-commerce-operator-tcs-collection-rate-reduced-0-25-percent.html).
  - The ECO may need a TCS registration in each state where its suppliers are located. [Karnataka TCS FAQ](https://gst.karnataka.gov.in/latestupdates/FAQsonTCS.pdf).
- **Creators below ₹20 lakh:** service suppliers selling through an ECO are **exempt from compulsory registration** up to ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover (Notification 65/2017-CT, 15 Nov 2017). The ECO then does **not** collect TCS on them. [Notification 65/2017](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/notfctn-65-central-tax-english.pdf); [CBIC TCS FAQ](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/FAQs-TCS-30-11-2018.pdf).
- **Section 9(5)** (the ECO pays tax as if it were the supplier) covers only *notified* services, such as passenger transport, accommodation and restaurants. Influencer services are not on the list. [GST Council e-commerce FAQ](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-e-commerc.pdf).
- **Effect on Mitra:**
  - If creator payouts are collected through Mitra's Route or Easy Split account, Mitra is very likely an ECO "collecting consideration". It would need **mandatory GST registration from day one** and must collect 0.5% TCS on supplies by GST-registered creators.
  - Whether a split-settlement setup where the PA collects counts as the "operator collecting" was **not found** in official guidance. Get advice.

### TDS (Income-tax Act 2025 in force from 1 Apr 2026, tax year 2026-27)

- **The new Act** (Act 30 of 2025) came into force on 1 Apr 2026. [Income-tax Act 2025 as amended by FA 2026](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf).
- **Sec 194-O becomes s.393(1) Table Sl. 8(v).** An ECO deducts **0.1%** of the gross amount of sales or services by an e-commerce participant (cut from 1% to 0.1% from 1 Oct 2024). [incometaxindia TDS FAQ](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/what-are-the-payments-covered-under-the-tds-mechanism-and-the-rates-for-deduction-of-tax-at-source-); [Audcrix](https://audcrix.com/income-tax-act-2025/section-194o); [TaxGuru chart, 1 May 2026](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/complete-tds-rate-chart-fy-2026-27-section-wise-breakdown.html).
  - **Threshold: sources conflict.** Audcrix says "Nil". Dhanaay says ₹5 lakh for individual or HUF participants, as under the old 194-O(4). [Dhanaay, 13 Apr 2026](https://dhanaay.com/s/tds-rate-chart). Verify against the text of the Act.
- **Sec 194R becomes s.393(1) Sl. 8(iv).** Anyone giving a business benefit or perquisite worth more than **₹20,000** in a year deducts **10%**. The duty falls on the business giving free products to creators, except an individual or HUF whose sales are under ₹1 cr. [TaxGuru chart](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/complete-tds-rate-chart-fy-2026-27-section-wise-breakdown.html).
  - CBDT Circular 12/2022 (16 Jun 2022): a product given to an influencer and **returned** is not a benefit; if **retained**, TDS applies. [CBDT Circular 12/2022](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/circular-no-12-2022-062fe002fcd64e9-62818365.pdf); [incometaxindia](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/tds-on-benefit-or-perquisite-arising-from-business-or-profession).
- **Sec 194J becomes s.393(1) Sl. 6(iii):** 10% on professional fees and 2% on technical fees, above ₹50,000 a year.
- **Sec 194C becomes s.393(1) Sl. 6(i):** 1% for individuals and 2% for others, above ₹30,000 single or ₹1 lakh total.
- Source for both: [TaxGuru chart, 1 May 2026](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/complete-tds-rate-chart-fy-2026-27-section-wise-breakdown.html).
- **Which section applies to a business paying a creator directly** (194J professional or 194C contract) depends on the facts. Whether the 194-O rule that excludes other TDS carries over into s.393 was **not verified**.

## 10. Advertising and data rules

### ASCI influencer guidelines

- **Issued May 2021.** Any **material connection** with a brand (cash, free products, perks) requires an upfront, hard-to-miss label.
- **Allowed labels:** Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, Affiliate, Instagram "Paid Partnership", YouTube "Includes Paid Promotion".
- **Placement and duration:**
  - The label cannot be buried in hashtags or kept only in the bio.
  - Videos of 15 seconds or less: at least 3 seconds.
  - 15 seconds to 2 minutes: one third of the length.
  - 2 minutes or more: for the whole section where the brand is mentioned.
  - Live streams: at the start and end.
- Source: [ASCI Influencer Guidelines PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf).
- **Health and finance (Addendum 2):**
  - Introduced 17 Aug 2023: finance and health influencers needed SEBI or other qualifications, stated upfront.
  - Updated 7 Apr 2025 (announced 28 Apr 2025): qualifications are needed **only for technical advice**, not generic promotion. [ASCI press release, 28 Apr 2025](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Press-Release_Influencer-Guidelines-Update.pdf); [Lexology, 5 May 2025](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=eb106940-efd6-406e-8073-909ff572f8af).
- **Compliance is low.** An ASCI study (6 Feb 2025) found 69% of the "Top 100 Digital Stars" broke the disclosure rules. [ASCI](https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Press-Release-Influencer-Disclosure-Guidelines-ASCI-Report.pdf).

### CCPA "Endorsement Know-hows!"

- **Issued 20 Jan 2023** by the Department of Consumer Affairs, under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and the Misleading Ads Guidelines of 9 Jun 2022.
- **Who must disclose:** celebrities, influencers and virtual influencers. Material benefits include money, trips, barter, free products, discounts and personal relationships.
- **How:** use "advertisement", "sponsored" or "paid promotion". Do not endorse anything not personally used.
- **Penalties:**
  - Up to **₹10 lakh**, and **₹50 lakh** for repeat offences.
  - Bans from endorsing for **1 year**, or **3 years** for repeat offences.
- Sources: [PIB, 20 Jan 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1892527); [DoCA PDF](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/filefield_paths/Endorsement_Know-Hows.pdf); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/product-promotions-govt-makes-disclosure-of-material-interest-mandatory-for-social-media-influencers/article66414009.ece).
- **Effect on Mitra:** Mitra's offers create the material connection. The safest course is to build disclosure into offer terms, for example a required #ad or paid-partnership tag, and keep records of it.

### DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

- **Rules notified 13 Nov 2025** (G.S.R. 846(E)); the Act's commencement was notified by G.S.R. 843(E).
- **Phases:**
  - **Immediately (13 Nov 2025):** the Data Protection Board and its procedures.
  - **12 months (13 Nov 2026):** Rule 4 on Consent Manager registration. Consent managers must be Indian companies with at least ₹2 cr net worth.
  - **18 months (13 May 2027):** the core duties. These cover notice (Rule 3), security, breach intimation, retention, children's data, data-principal rights and the Significant Data Fiduciary rules.
- Sources: [Rules text](https://www.dpdpa.in/dpdpa_rules_2025/dpdpa_rules_english_2.pdf); [PIB, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&lang=1&reg=3); [JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/JSA-Prism-InfoTech-November-2025-DPDP-Rules.Final_.pdf); [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa).
- **Proposal to shorten the timeline.** In January 2026 MeitY proposed cutting it to 12 months, with some rules taking effect immediately or within 3 months. Industry pushed back by 4 Feb 2026. **No amending notification was found.**
  - A Lok Sabha answer of **12 Aug 2026** still states the 18-month timeline.
  - A Removal of Difficulties Order (6 Oct 2026) made only wording changes and did not move any dates.
  - Sources: [Business Standard, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html); [HT, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/industry-groups-asks-it-ministry-to-not-shorten-compliance-timeline-under-dpdp-101770233287416.html); [LS USQ 3943, 12 Aug 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3943_458sox.pdf); [dpdprules.org timeline, checked 18 Oct 2026](https://dpdprules.org/timeline).
- **Duties from 13 May 2027 for an ordinary data fiduciary such as Mitra:**
  - A standalone, plain-language notice listing the data and purposes, with an easy way to withdraw consent.
  - Notices available in 22 Indian languages, per the Lok Sabha answer.
  - Contracts with data processors.
  - Reasonable security, such as encryption or masking.
  - **Breach reporting:** to the Board and affected users without delay, and a detailed report within **72 hours**.
  - Grievance replies within **90 days**.
  - Erasure when consent is withdrawn.
  - At least 1 year of log retention.
  - **Verifiable parental consent for users under 18**, which matters because many creators are minors.
- **Penalties:** up to **₹250 cr**.
- Sources: [KPMG DPDP Rules guide](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/dpdp-rules-2025-guidance-to-dpdp-act-implementation.pdf); [LS USQ 4091](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU4091_ajtbM6.pdf); [LS USQ 3943](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3943_458sox.pdf).
- **Data Protection Board status.** The Kerala High Court (7 Oct 2026) recorded that the Board has been constituted but is "yet to be made functional", and expected it to function within 8 weeks (about 2 Dec 2026). [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/timeline).
- **Effect on Mitra:** the per-customer bonus means tracking end customers, which is personal data. So consent and notice flows, plus the retention and breach plans, must be ready by **13 May 2027**.
