## 1. Indian comparables

### magicpin (closest Indian comparable: local offers for merchants)
| Field | Fact |
|---|---|
| Founded | Aug 2015, Gurugram, by Anshoo Sharma (ex-Lightspeed) and Brij Bhushan (ex-Nexus) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magicpin) |
| Wedge | Users photographed a bill at a partner outlet and earned rewards (recharges, vouchers). Bills were also accepted over WhatsApp. Cashback discovery for offline stores [Medianama, Feb 2016](https://www.medianama.com/2016/02/223-magicpin-secures-3million/) |
| Early merchant pricing | Merchants paid a subscription fee to join and then 12–15% of revenue driven by magicpin (Series B era, c.2017) [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1497517) |
| Series A | Feb 2016, $3M, Lightspeed (which had incubated it) [Inc42](https://inc42.com/flash-feed/magicpin-funding) |
| Series B | $7M, Lightspeed and others [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/magicpin-series-b-lightspeed) |
| Later rounds | $20M from Lightspeed US [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=141163). Valuation about $100M in Nov 2018 and about $147M in May 2020. Ritesh Agarwal-led round in Aug 2021 at $165M post-money [Entrackr, Aug 2021](https://entrackr.com/2021/08/exclusive-ritesh-agarwal-leads-fresh-investment-in-magicpin-at-165-mn-valuation). $60M Series D led by Zomato and Lightspeed in 2021 [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/savings-startup-magicpin-raises-60-mn-from-zoma...). Third venture-debt raise of ₹15 cr from Stride Ventures in Apr 2026 [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/magicpin-1). Caplight estimates a $312M valuation (third-party estimate) |
| Metrics | 2021: 1.5 lakh merchants, 50 lakh consumers, 50 cities. Commission 10% on F&B and up to 7% on pharma and grocery [Entrackr, 2021](https://entrackr.com/?p=108996). FY23 revenue ₹297 cr. FY24 revenue ₹870 cr, **92% from voucher sales** [Entrackr, FY24 filing](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/magicpin-triples-revenue-to-rs-870-cr-in-fy24-cuts-losses-8710385). FY24 net loss ₹107 cr per [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/magicpin/financials/), ₹78 cr per [Lapaas](https://lapaasvoice.com/startup/magicpin/financials/). Adjusted EBITDA margin −9.8% in FY24 vs −39.2% in FY23, "unit-level profitable", breakeven targeted within 6 months [YourStory, Feb 2025](https://yourstory.com/2025/02/magicpin-clocks-multifold-growth-fy24-improved-profitability). FY25 fashion GMV about ₹1,000 cr across 16,000 stores [Business Standard, Apr 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/magicpin-fashion-verticals-clock-20-turnover-at-rs-1-000-crore-in-fy25-125041100542_1.html). Consolidated FY25 revenue: not found |
- Lesson: magicpin's revenue scaled only after it turned offers into **voucher sales**: the consumer pays magicpin up front and magicpin keeps the margin. The merchant subscription plus commission model did not drive the growth. Ten years in and with about $110M raised, it is still not profitable. Local offers in India take a long time and a lot of capital, and much of the volume shifted to food delivery (magicNOW). Mitra's coupon code sits close to magicpin's turf. Its difference has to be the creator as the distribution channel.

- Started as SoSasta. Groupon acquired it in 2011 and rebranded it Groupon India in 2013. Management buyout and rebrand to nearbuy in Aug 2015, with $17M from Sequoia [exchange4media, 2015](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/groupon-india-gets-funding-from-sequoia-indiarebrands-itself-as-'nearbuy'-61180.html). Paytm bought Nearbuy and Little in Dec 2017 in what was reported as a "distress sale" [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/atom/paytm-set-buy-deal-platforms-nearbuy-and-little-distressed-sale-71551). ### Zomato (early restaurant listings)
- Launched as Foodiebay in 2008 and renamed Zomato in 2010. It was a free directory of menus and reviews paid for by restaurant ads, about ₹12,000 a month for a typical ad. Co-founder Pankaj Chaddah: "I was the only sales person on the ground." Early reach was Gurgaon and South Delhi [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/big-idea/serving-it-hot-37), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/what-is-zomatos-business-model/).
- Lesson: start with **consumer-side content for free**, then sell to restaurants once there is audience proof, one locality at a time with founders selling in person.

### Thrive (restaurant direct ordering, shut down)
- Founded 2020. Commission-light direct ordering: claimed 3% against 18–25% on aggregators. Jubilant FoodWorks took 35% for about ₹25 cr in 2021. Coca-Cola took 15% at a ₹104.68 cr pre-money in 2023. 14,000+ restaurants in 80 cities. **Shut down** with 42 staff, citing "a few well-funded giants" and difficulty getting consumers to switch from Zomato and Swiggy [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/coca-cola-backed-foodtech-startup-thrive-shuts-operations), [Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/coca-cola-acquires-15%25-stake-in-food-tech-platform-thrive).
- Lesson: **restaurants sign up for cheaper tools, but consumers drive the demand.** If Mitra relies on the merchant to bring its own customers, it inherits Thrive's problem. Creators have to deliver the customers.

### Groupon (merchant economics and collapse)
- Grew out of The Point (2007) and launched in Chicago in 2008. 2010 revenue was $713M (22.7x growth), but there was a $420M operating loss once the ACSOI metric was dropped. It raised $1.12B of VC before its IPO [TechCrunch, Jun 2011](https://techcrunch.com/2011/06/02/groupon-files-for-ipo/). Market cap was about $13–17.8B at the Nov 2011 IPO and later fell to about $103M, down 99.4% [Richmond case](https://robins.richmond.edu/files/Robins-Case-Network/2025/Groupon.pdf), [Slashdot/TechCrunch, Mar 2023](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/23/03/31/1753253).
- Merchant economics: deal sites took 30–50% of the discounted price on top of a roughly 50% discount. In Rice University's Dholakia study (2010–11), about 32% of merchants lost money (some write-ups say 42% for restaurants). **Only about 1 in 5 deal buyers came back at full price.** Salons and spas did best and restaurants did worst [MIT Tech Review, Oct 2010](https://www.technologyreview.com/2010/10/25/199737/social-coupons-good-for-business/amp/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/content/forty-percent-groupon-merchants-say-never-again). Groupon later had to cut its take rate to keep merchants [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/internet/32519/groupon-in-troubled-waters/).

### Toast
- Founded 2011 in Boston (ex-Endeca). The first product, a layer on top of existing POS, failed, and it pivoted to an Android cloud POS in 2013. Founders went door to door to restaurants [MIT News, Mar 2019](https://news.mit.edu/2019/toast-restaurants-0310). Series B Jan 2016: $30M led by Bessemer with GV [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-raises-30-million-funding-to-fuel-growth). Series A details: not reported.
- S-1 (Aug 2021): 47,942 locations at 30 Jun 2021 (19,891 in 2019). ARR $494M. GPV over $38B TTM. 2020 revenue about $823M, of which fintech was about $644M (about 78%; my calculation from a summary) [SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650164/000119312521258447/d166297ds1.htm), [FoodOnDemand](https://foodondemand.com/09022021/toast-pos-set-to-go-public/).

## 3. (a) Indian SMB SaaS churn and willingness to pay
- Khatabook FY24: ₹102.7 cr revenue against a ₹116 cr loss, with millions of merchants. OkCredit FY25: ₹23.3 cr revenue. Dukaan FY23: ₹10 cr revenue from 2.7M+ merchant sign-ups (sources above). Free merchant utilities brought in very little revenue per merchant.
- Indian SMBs are highly price-sensitive. Their SaaS competes with Excel, they prefer flat yearly pricing, and RBI e-mandate/2FA rules complicate card auto-debit [Embedworkflow](https://embedworkflow.com/blog/selling-saas-in-india/). Indian SaaS vendors self-reported about 10% annual churn, probably under-reported [iSPIRT survey, 2017](https://pn.ispirt.in/india-saas-survey-2017-results/). I found **no public churn data for Indian micro-SMB SaaS.** Mitra should measure its own.
- The paying exceptions are operations-critical tools: Petpooja (1 lakh+ restaurants) and Toast-style POS where payments carry the revenue.

## 3. (b) Groupon and magicpin lessons on local offer economics
- Groupon: a roughly 50% discount plus a 30–50% platform cut meant the merchant kept about 25% of list price. About 20% of deal buyers came back at full price, and 32–42% of merchants lost money. Restaurants did worst and salons and spas did best (sources above).
- magicpin: its early merchant subscription plus 12–15% commission did not scale. Revenue reached ₹870 cr in FY24 once it sold vouchers to consumers directly (92% of revenue), and it is still loss-making after 10 years (sources above).
- Takeaway for Mitra: keep the discount shallow, charge the merchant per **new** customer, report repeat visits, and start with salons ahead of cafés.
