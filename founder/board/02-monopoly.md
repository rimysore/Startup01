# Monopoly lens
Score: 2/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **Timing is real.** Agent Skills became an open standard in Dec 2025 and ~32 tools adopted it by March 2026 (brief). Buyers pay for agents that finish a job (Sierra, Harvey).
- **The founder has relevant skills.** Toolslim shows agent-infrastructure skill; cost-to-serve is the one place a technical edge could matter.
- **Trust is a real gap.** About 12-13% of public skills are malicious or critically flawed (Snyk, Koi, in the brief). A tested, sandboxed, guaranteed worker answers a real fear.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Durability: no moat at all.** The supply is MIT-licensed markdown that anyone can copy (brief). Every "worker" is a public skill on someone else's model. Nothing is proprietary, let alone ten times better. A copycat needs a weekend, not six months. Rating and test data is the only possible asset, and only at volume.
2. **Distribution is owned by the platforms you depend on.** Anthropic's Claude Marketplace reportedly charges no commission. ChatGPT has a unified apps directory. Upwork shipped an MCP server so AI tools can hire through it (Aug 2026), and said it is extending its marketplace to AI agents, with a human-supervised agent offering in beta in Q2 and rolling out to all customers this year ([Upwork](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upwork-talent-now-everywhere-ai-works), [Q1 call](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/upwork-upwk-q1-2026-earnings-153307037.html)). monday.com launched Agentalent.ai in March 2026, a vetted marketplace for hiring agents into business roles that it built with AWS and Anthropic. Constellation notes ServiceNow launched something similar ([Constellation](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/monday-launches-ai-agent-marketplace), [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/monday-com-launches-ai-agent-hiring-platform-agentalent-ai)). AgenticHire is already live ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/agentichire)). The pitch has no answer to how the first 100 customers arrive or what each one costs.
3. **This is a crowded market, not a small one you can own.** A horizontal catalog of every skill means fighting model owners, Upwork, Fiverr, monday and dozens of directories for thin margins. The GPT Store mostly failed to pay builders and HubSpot folded Agent.ai into its CRM (brief). Platform risk already killed the closest comparables.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know that Anthropic, OpenAI, Upwork and monday do not? If the answer is "skills are good," that is no secret. Everyone in the brief already acts on it.
- Name the single job and buyer you would own first. Can you be the obvious first choice for, say, 500 customers within 12 months?
- What is ten times better: price per finished task, quality as measured by your own evals, or a guarantee? Show the benchmark.
- How do the first 100 paying customers find you, and what does each cost? (Open question, since the brief has no CAC data. `/founder-competitors` and `/founder-cfo`.)
- What stops Anthropic from adding a "hire" button to the Claude Marketplace next quarter?
- Why would skill authors list with you rather than on a zero-commission first-party marketplace? (Open question. The brief shows install counts are tiny, e.g. 767 installs across 162 skills.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable, since the vote is PASS. What would change my vote:
- Drop the horizontal marketplace. Pick one job, such as security audits for small SaaS teams or spreadsheet and finance ops for small businesses, and sell finished outcomes rather than agents.
- Show a proprietary asset that builds up: your own eval and test corpus for that job, outcome data, or integrations a skill file cannot copy.
- Name a distribution channel you control, with a measured cost per customer from at least 20 paying customers.
- State a secret in one sentence that the platform owners would dismiss.

## Market ceiling
As pitched, this is a **million-dollar company at best**, and more likely zero. A horizontal catalog of free, copyable skills sits downstream of platforms that give the same thing away. The bigger version is a vertical "service-as-software" company that owns one job end to end, prices on outcomes and builds proprietary evals and workflow data. Sierra and Harvey show that shape can reach **billion-dollar** scale, but that would be a different company from this pitch. A trillion-dollar outcome belongs to the model platforms, not to a catalog that sits on top of them.
