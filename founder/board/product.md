# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pitch fits in one sentence: it reads your own channel and tells you, in your language, what to post next and why your last video did what it did. Most products at this stage can't say that.
- Starting from the creator's own analytics, not generic tips, is the right place to start the experience.
- The Monday report and the 2-day post-upload check go to the creator. They don't have to remember to open a dashboard. This could be the real product.
- The AI cost per action is known down to the feature, so the founders have looked at the parts nobody sees.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The headline feature may break platform rules.** The outlier ("×-your-usual") scores look like the "derived metrics" that YouTube API policy forbids without the Analytics & Reporting amendment, and that amendment hasn't been submitted. Google OAuth verification is also still pending, so a new creator's first screen may be Google's "unverified app" warning. Both seams belong to Google, not Mitra, and both sit in the first five minutes. If either one fails, the product loses its core feature or its sign-up flow.
2. **No focus.** There are ten-plus surfaces (overview, Video Doctor, Title & Thumbnail Studio, Trend Radar, comments/slang, competitors, breakout ideas, Ask Mitra, reports, agency tier), plus a credit system, for one active tester and zero paying customers. Nobody has proven which one thing creators come back for. Credits make it worse: before getting value, the creator has to work out what each action costs.
3. **The experience has open seams.** Billing is off, WhatsApp (the promised push channel) is waiting on a business number, and the app hands work between a full-time engineer in US Eastern time and a part-time co-founder in IST who owns customer conversations. Without a closed loop of sign-up, first insight, report, payment and follow-up, nobody can find out whether the product is good.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you could keep only one feature, which would it be? What evidence says creators want that one?
- Walk me through a new Telugu creator's first five minutes, screen by screen, including the Google consent screen as it looks today.
- Do the outlier scores count as derived metrics under YouTube policy? What does the product become if the answer is yes and the amendment isn't granted?
- Has Ujjwal changed what he posted because of a Mitra report? How many times?
- Are the regional-language outputs native and correct? Who checks quality in each of the eight languages, and how?
- What do you lose if you cut credits and sell one plan?
- What's the open rate on the Monday email so far? (Unknown. This is an open question.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Submit the Analytics & Reporting amendment and get OAuth verification. Until then, remove or redesign any feature that depends on derived metrics.
2. Cut to one core loop: connect channel, get a weekly report, get the post-upload check. Hide everything else behind it or drop it. Launch in one or two languages, not eight.
3. Turn on Razorpay and run the full loop end to end with real creators. The minimum bar is 10 paying creators at ₹99, with month-2 retention measured and reported back.
4. Before scaling, have a native speaker spot-check the reports in every language you launch.
