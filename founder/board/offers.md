# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers are easy to find.** Every target customer publishes publicly on YouTube, with language and subscriber count visible. Prospecting is cheap and precise.
- **The dream outcome is vivid.** "Grow and get monetised" is the one thing these creators want, and it is a big outcome.
- **The effort asked is low.** A Monday report pushed to them (WhatsApp later), written in their language and based on their own channel, asks almost nothing of the buyer. That is the right shape for an offer.
- **Delivery is cheap.** Typical AI cost is ~₹23 on a ₹82 net Pro sale. There is room to stack more value without breaking the margin.
- **The scarcity is real.** "First 100 creators" is an honest limit, not a fake one.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is no proof, so the perceived likelihood is near zero.** 0 paying customers and one active tester. The offer promises growth, but no creator has a before/after yet. Without one, ₹99 is a bet on an unknown app, sold against free YouTube Studio and ChatGPT the buyer already uses.
2. **The pitch is about price, and the price undercuts the business.** "India pricing (₹99)" is listed as an edge. Under this lens, being cheapest is a commodity position. Break-even needs 35–70 Pro creators, before any salary. At ₹99 there is no budget to acquire customers and no margin for a guarantee. And the 1K-subscriber end of the market may have little money to spend at all.
3. **The time to a result is long, and the platform controls access.** A "growth" outcome takes weeks to months to show, so month-1 churn is likely before value lands. On top of that, the core product (outlier scores are derived metrics) waits on Google OAuth verification and an amendment that has not been submitted. If YouTube ships regional-language insights for free, the dream outcome is available elsewhere at zero cost.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which subscriber band has the money and the pain? Are 1K-sub hobbyists the same buyer as 100K-sub creators who earn from the channel? What share of the target list is monetised? (Unknown.)
- Do you have one creator whose numbers moved after following Mitra's advice? If not, when will you?
- How fast is the first win? Can a creator get one useful insight within 5 minutes of connecting, before any growth shows up?
- What is the guarantee? For example: "Act on 4 weekly reports; if your views do not beat your usual, the next month is free."
- Could you charge ₹499–999 to monetising creators and still be the obvious choice? What would the stack need for that (a human review call, a title rewrite every week, a brand-deal readiness check)?
- How many creators can the two of you reach each week, and what share convert? (Unknown. These two numbers set whether this is a business.)
- What is the timeline for submitting the derived-metrics amendment, and what is the product without it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Turn billing on and get 10 paying creators within 60 days**, with no lifetime freebies counted. That tests the open question of whether creators pay at all.
2. **Document 3 creator case studies** with before/after channel numbers, and put them at the front of the offer.
3. **Rebuild the offer around the outcome, not the price.** Write a named promise with a guarantee. Test a higher tier for monetising creators (for example 50K+ subscribers), and stop leading with ₹99.
4. **Submit the YouTube Analytics & Reporting amendment now**, and confirm the core features are compliant before spending on outreach.
5. **Track month-1 retention and creators reached per week**, and report both back to the board.
