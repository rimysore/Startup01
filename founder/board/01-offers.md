# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS (as pitched)

## What is strong
- The macro market is real. Money is moving from salaries and outsourced services to software (Foundation Capital's $4.6T "service-as-software" framing, per the brief), and the agents that are winning sell a finished outcome, not a tool. "Hire a worker, get finished work" is the right shape for an offer.
- Supply costs nothing. Open-source skills are free and number in the tens of thousands or more, so the founder never pays to build the catalog.
- Buyers already pay for small outcomes. Fiverr buyers spent $342 each in 2025 (Fiverr Q4/FY2025 release: https://investors.fiverr.com/node/9886/pdf), and Devin sells about 15 minutes of autonomous engineering for roughly $2.00-2.25 (third-party pricing pages: https://pricepertoken.com/coding-assistants/devin).

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No market has been picked, and the market you get by default is weak.** "People and companies" is not a market. The default buyer, a solo founder moving off Fiverr, spends about $342 a year, and that pool is shrinking (3.1M active Fiverr buyers, down 13.6%, same release). Buyers with real pain and real budgets (legal, support, sales) are already served by vertical companies such as Sierra, Harvey, 11x and Artisan, which sell one job end to end.
2. **It is a commodity offer.** The product is a free markdown file running on a model the buyer can already reach. Skills run natively in Claude, ChatGPT/Codex, Cursor and VS Code, and Anthropic's marketplace is reportedly commission-free. A user who knows about skills installs ui-ux-pro-max for free; a user who does not cannot judge why your "xlsx worker" beats asking ChatGPT. The only thing left to compete on is price, and you lose that against the companies that own the models.
3. **Buyers have no reason to believe it works, and they have to do work to use it.** Proof is zero: installs are tiny (767 total across one 162-skill publisher), about 13% of scanned skills have critical issues (Snyk) and about 12% of one hub's skills were malicious (Koi). Asking a buyer to "connect files/repo/tools" to an unaccountable bot is a large effort and sacrifice. Small pay-per-task agent marketplaces show what happens: ActMyAgent lists $2k MRR (https://peerpush.com/p/actmyagent), and Bounty reports 1,300 posted tasks on $1M of funding (https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=46086). Both self-reported; neither proves pull.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one buyer, with which one painful job, already pays a human $500 or more to do it?
- Why would that buyer pick your agent over Claude or ChatGPT with the same free skill installed?
- What do you guarantee? If the work is wrong, do you refund it, redo it, or have a human fix it?
- How fast is a usable result, and who checks it before it ships?
- Could you charge 3x Fiverr for this job and still be the obvious choice? If not, why not?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not FUND IF as pitched. These would flip the vote to FUND IF:
1. Kill the catalog. Pick one job for one buyer with money (my pick: a security audit and fix-up PR for small SaaS teams, where pain and budget exist and the skill-security crisis is your proof of need). Run one worker that is hardened and benchmarked.
2. Sell the outcome with a guarantee: "audit plus fixes in 24 hours, or you pay nothing," with a human reviewer in the loop. Price against the human, not tokens.
3. Stack it: scoped access, written report, re-test after fixes, compliance summary.
4. Show proof before scaling: 10 paying customers at $500 or more each, and at least 3 who come back. Unknowns for `/founder-consumer`, `/founder-pricing` and `/founder-cfo`: willingness to pay per task, CAC, and compute cost per job.

## Market ceiling
As pitched, a horizontal skill-agent catalog is a million-dollar company at best. It is a feature the model owners are already giving away. A version that sells one guaranteed outcome to buyers with budgets could be a $100M+ business, and vertical leaders show that billion-dollar outcomes exist (Sierra $15B+, Harvey $2.1B, per the brief). But that is a different company from the one pitched.
