# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **One part does something nobody nearby does.** The customer counter answers the owner's own complaint: "I give free meals to creators and have no idea if anyone walks in." Cost per customer per creator, checked against the bill book, is a clear result an owner can see. It is the most likely reason the business panel says yes more often (55%) than the creator panel (30%).
- **The local fit is real.** Kannada and English throughout, ranked to the creator's own area ("In Jayanagar, your area"), starting in two neighbourhoods. That is a focused place to start.
- **Small touches that build trust.** "Verified" numbers, the reasons shown next to each match, the daily sheet to check against the bill book, the scam check. These are the parts nobody asked for that show care.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The counting moment breaks where staff and customers take over.** The whole promise depends on a staff member typing a PIN during the lunch rush and a customer remembering to show a coupon. Both panels raised this as their top doubt. If counts read low, the owner decides creators do not work, and the one thing that sets Mitra apart turns into proof against it.
2. **Too many products at once, and none finished end to end.** A marketplace, a customer counter, Auto-DM, affiliate programmes, a scam check, a media kit and a Creator Pass, all before one user. The parts that matter most are not ready: Meta app not set up (so no Verified numbers, no Auto-DM), payment not built (an admin switches it on by hand after a UPI payment), no escrow, no registered company to complain to. Each gap is a seam the user will see.
3. **The creator's first five minutes may be an empty room.** With zero businesses listed, "Collabs for you" shows little, and what it does show is mostly barter. Creators said they pay only for a cash collab and doubt anyone is on it. Influish already sells Auto-DM and creator tools at the same ₹499, so the creator side has no clear one thing of its own.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is Mitra? Is it "the way a Bengaluru café knows which creator brought customers in"? If not, what is it?
- Walk me through the first coupon redemption at a busy darshini at 1pm: who does what, how long does it take, and what happens when the staff skip it?
- What share of real visits actually get counted? Nobody has this number yet; how will the pilot measure it against the bill book?
- What would you cut before launch? Could Auto-DM, affiliate programmes and the Creator Pass wait until the counter works?
- Can the business side work over WhatsApp with no new login, as the panel asked?
- Who sets up each business in person, and who does a creator or owner call when something goes wrong?
- What does a creator see on day one if only a handful of businesses are listed?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick the one thing: the customer counter for local businesses.** Launch with that and the matching that feeds it; put Auto-DM, affiliate programmes and the paid pass behind it.
2. **Make redeeming effortless at the counter**, tested in person at a few real outlets during a rush, before the wider launch. Report the counted share against the bill book as the pilot's main number.
3. **Close the trust gaps the user sees:** a named, reachable person or company, a way to report a non-paying business, and Meta App Review started (or the "Verified" label kept off until it passes).
4. **Fill the room before inviting creators:** enough businesses listed in the two neighbourhoods, including some cash collabs, so a creator's first visit is not empty. How many is enough is an open question the pilot should answer.
