# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **One side has a real, measurable pain.** Local owners already give free meals to creators and cannot tell if anyone walks in. The customer counter (coupon link, PIN redeem, cost per customer, daily sheet to check against the bill book) answers that pain directly. 11 of 20 simulated owners said yes, even for a free tool.
- **The buyers are easy to find.** Two neighbourhoods, walkable cafés, darshinis, salons and gyms, and creators who list their area on Instagram. Reach is cheap and can be done in person.
- **The stack fixes real problems on the creator side.** Verified numbers, collabs ranked to area and niche, Auto-DM that turns comments into counted customers, and a free scam check. Each one solves a problem a creator hits on the way to a paid collab.
- **It is not only a lower price.** Against Influish (same ₹499/year), the local focus and proof of customers per post are a real difference, not a discount.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The creator's dream outcome (a cash collab) has no proof and low likelihood.** Zero users, zero businesses, and the panel says local cafés pay in food. Creators are asked to pay ₹499 for access to a marketplace that may be empty, which is the exact complaint in Influish's public reviews. Trust was the pass reason for 10 of 14 creators.
2. **The side with the pain and the money pays nothing, and the side that pays has the least money.** Businesses get the most valuable part free; creators with 2K-100K followers are asked to pay, and their bargain price clusters at ₹199. The market test (massive pain plus money to spend) passes for businesses and is weak for creators. How this earns enough is unanswered.
3. **The proof engine may read low.** Both yes and no owners doubt that staff will redeem PINs in a rush or that customers will show a coupon. If counts come in low, the core promise (count the customers each post brings) looks like a failure even when the post worked. Effort is put on the busiest person in the shop.

Also blocking: no Meta app yet, so Verified numbers and Auto-DM (two stack items) are not available to real users at launch; no registered company, so no escrow and no answer to "who do I complain to".

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many businesses in the two neighbourhoods will pay creators cash (not barter) in the first month, and how will you know before creators are asked to pay?
- What happens to a creator who pays ₹499 and gets no paid collab? Is there a guarantee?
- Why are businesses free when they hold the pain and the budget? What would they pay per counted customer?
- What share of real customers show the coupon and get redeemed? (Unknown until a pilot runs.)
- When will the Meta app be submitted, and what does launch look like if review takes longer than 1-2 weeks?
- Who is the named company or person a creator can report a non-paying business to?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Supply before the paywall:** a set number of businesses (founder to name it) live in the two neighbourhoods with cash briefs posted before any creator is asked for ₹499.
2. **A guarantee the founder can afford:** e.g. no paid collab within a stated period, the pass is refunded or extended. Name it plainly.
3. **Prove the counter in person:** someone from Mitra sets up a few shops, and real redeemed coupons are matched to the bill book over a pilot period. Publish that result as the proof both sides asked for.
4. **Test a business price:** offer the counter (or a per-customer fee) to pilot owners once counts are proven, to find whether the side with money will pay.
5. **Fix trust basics:** a named operator, a way to report non-payment, and the Meta app submitted before launch.
