# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **It starts small on purpose.** One city, two neighbourhoods, Kannada and English. The right shape: a market small enough to own first.
- **The customer counter could be proprietary.** It links a creator's post to the customers who walk in, checked against the owner's bill book. That answers the doubt barter-veteran owners say out loud ("I give free meals and have no idea if anyone walks in"). Influish's public material says nothing about counting offline customers.
- **A local network effect is possible.** Each café that counts customers makes nearby creators more valuable, and each proven creator brings in more cafés. Packed into a few streets, this compounds; spread thin, it does not.
- **There is a candidate secret:** small Bengaluru businesses pay creators in food and cannot measure the result, and national apps built for brand campaigns do not serve them.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Empty marketplace.** Zero users on either side. Creators' top doubt is "is it empty?", and the brief does not say how the first businesses and creators arrive or what each costs. With no distribution, there is no business.
2. **Counts that read low.** Owners on both the yes and no sides doubt that staff will redeem the PIN in a rush or that customers will show a coupon. If it undercounts, the one differentiator becomes proof that creators do not work.
3. **Nothing stops a copycat, and the creator offer is the competitor's offer.** Auto-DM, 0% commission and ₹499 a year are what Influish already sells, with a claimed 1.5M+ creators and escrow already working. Coupon links with a PIN are easy to copy. The core features also depend on Meta's API and App Review, which has not been started.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which two neighbourhoods, how many target businesses are in them, and what share must count customers before that market is "owned"?
- How do the first 20 businesses and 100 creators arrive, and what does each cost? (Open question: no acquisition cost exists yet.)
- In a live rush, what share of real walk-ins get redeemed? (Open question: no measured rate exists.)
- Why charge creators, the side with the trust doubts and a same-price alternative, while businesses, the side with the measurement pain, pay nothing?
- What will Mitra know after six months that Influish could not copy in six months?
- What happens if Meta rejects or limits Auto-DM?
- When will the company be registered, and who does a creator contact if a business does not pay?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Own one neighbourhood first:** a named list of target businesses, a target share counting customers weekly, tracked in admin.
2. **Prove the counter at the till:** a pilot that matches redeemed coupons to the bill book, with a measured redemption rate the owners accept.
3. **Lead with the counter, not Auto-DM.** Position Mitra as "count the customers a creator brings", not a second Auto-DM app at the same price.
4. **Write the first-100 plan:** who on the ground signs people up, and the cost per business and per creator.
5. **Fix trust before charging:** register the company, publish a way to report a non-paying business, and land at least one cash collab in the free month that a creator will vouch for.
6. **Remove the Meta single point of failure:** start App Review now, and make sure the counter and collabs work without it.
