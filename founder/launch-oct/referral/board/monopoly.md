# Monopoly lens
Score: 2 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **It targets the real bottleneck: distribution.** The previous board said fill the business side first; this idea at least asks how the first businesses and creators arrive, and recruits through local counters rather than ads.
- **The local-invite channel itself works.** In the simulated creator panel, most creators would still take the free first collab because a café they know asked. That is a distribution asset in a small, nameable market.
- **The underlying moat candidate is untouched.** The counted-customer record at local counters is the one thing the competitor map says Influish cannot copy quickly.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It moves Mitra into the most crowded square on the map.** Paying referrers a share of a ₹499 creator pass is exactly what Influish already does, at a higher rate (₹250, 50% vs ₹150, ~30%), with a claimed 1.5M creators. Competing on referral payouts is competing on price, where the incumbent outbids at will. No proprietary edge, only a smaller copy.
2. **It erodes the only brand Mitra could build: trust.** Creator buy intent fell from 6/20 to 0/20 in the simulated panel, with trust the top reason (11). Money flowing from the creator to the business that invited them matches the public definition of the registration-fee scam, and Mitra's own scam checker flags that pattern. A local marketplace's durability rests on being the trusted place; this spends that before it exists.
3. **It costs money and buys nothing.** Business sign-ups were unchanged (11/20 with or without it), several said ₹150/₹200 "don't move" them, and trust passes rose (3 to 5). Add TDS, PAN collection, GST advice and a registered company that does not yet exist, and the cost per extra business acquired looks unbounded. Distribution that adds cost but not customers is not distribution.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the small market you intend to own first, and what share of its cafés would you need before creators come on their own?
- What is your secret: what do you know about Bengaluru counters that Influish does not?
- If Influish raised its referral to ₹300 tomorrow, what of this idea would still be yours?
- How do the first 100 businesses arrive, and what does each cost, with and without the ₹200 a month?
- Who funds the ₹200 a month per business, for how long, and what happens to retention when it stops? (Budget is not stated.)
- How would you stop referral gaming (fake creators, self-referrals) before you have a company to enforce terms?

## A better version of the idea, if this lens sees one (one short paragraph)
Keep the channel, drop the payment from the creator. Own one neighbourhood first: recruit cafés in person, and let each café's invite bring creators to a free, counted collab. Reward businesses (if at all) from results they can see on the customer counter, such as a featured slot or a verified "customers counted" badge, not from creators' passes. The defensible asset is a dense, verified record of which local creators bring real walk-ins to which counters; that compounds with every redeemed coupon (a data and network effect within a small market) and is something a pan-India Auto-DM app cannot copy by changing a referral rate. Open question: whether staff will actually redeem PINs in a rush, since that record is the whole moat.
