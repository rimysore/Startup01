# Operations: the referral model vs the board's alternative (October 2026)

What it takes to run each version day to day, and what that costs. Real prices found here have been fed back into `cfo.md` (gateway fee with GST, payouts, compliance, rep pay, print). Not legal or tax advice: rules differ by place and change; confirm each item below with the authority or a CA.

## 1. The daily cycle

**Both versions** (customer-visible steps marked ★):

1. Rep visits 4–6 shops in the target neighbourhood (morning, before the 12–2 pm rush).
2. ★ Set up a new shop: listing, offer, counter PIN, one test redemption, standee on the counter.
3. ★ Creators sign up from a café's invite link or a WhatsApp group; their first collab is free.
4. ★ Customers show coupons at the counter; staff redeem.
5. Afternoon: call or WhatsApp yesterday's shops ("did staff redeem? anything stuck?").
6. Evening: check admin (pilot numbers, members), reply to support messages.
7. Weekly: bill-book check at 3 shops; Friday numbers review.

**Extra steps only in the referral model (B):**

8. Attribute each paid pass to the referring business (first link wins, 60-day window).
9. Hold the commission 30 days (refund window), check for self-referrals, then pay out.
10. Monthly: pay ₹200 to each "active" business after checking activity, deduct TDS once a payee's annual total crosses the threshold, keep PAN on file, file TDS returns quarterly.
11. Track and chase the "promo videos" each pass holder owes, and handle disputes about them.

Steps 8–11 are new back-office work that touches money every day. Plan C replaces them with step 12:

12. **Results guarantee (C):** if a shop's first creator post brings fewer than the agreed number of counted customers in 30 days, the rep finds it a second creator and sets up that post at no charge.

## 4. Routines (one page each)

### A. Setting up a shop (15 minutes, before 11 am)
1. Introduce yourself with a leaflet; say what it is in one line (counter-first, Kannada first).
2. On the owner's phone: Google sign-in → business profile (name, category, area) → listed.
3. Set the counter PIN with the owner; write it nowhere public.
4. Create the first offer (e.g. "10% off your bill") and the first tracked post if a creator is lined up.
5. Do one test redemption with the staff member who'll be at the counter.
6. Put the standee next to the cash counter, facing customers.
7. Give the owner your name and WhatsApp number. Book a call for the next day.

### B. Redeeming at the counter during the rush (the step every panel doubted)
1. A customer shows the coupon on their phone.
2. Staff type the 4-digit PIN on the customer's screen and tap redeem: one step, under 5 seconds.
3. If it's too busy: staff note the 6-character code on the bill and enter it later in **counter mode** (already built), within the day.
4. End of day: the owner glances at "today's customers" and compares it with the bill book.

**Fix to test before launch:** remove the PIN step for staff who are busy, e.g. the customer's screen shows a code that staff simply write on the bill, or a one-tap "counted" at the counter. Measure the counted share at 3 shops (`marketing.md`, number 1).

### C. A business invites creators (the channel the founder wants, without money)
1. The owner opens "Invite creators" and shares the link on WhatsApp with creators who already DM them.
2. The creator signs up from the link; the café is pre-selected as their first, free collab.
3. The rep checks the deal room the next day and helps both sides agree terms (fee, barter or customer bonus).
4. The post goes live with the coupon link; the count starts.
*(Plan B adds: attribution, a 30-day hold, a commission payout, a PAN request and TDS. Plan C adds nothing here.)*

### D. A complaint ("the count is wrong" / "the business didn't pay")
1. Reply the same day with a real name.
2. Ask for the day, the creator and the bill-book numbers (or the deal room for a payment dispute).
3. Compare with the redemption log within a day; say what you found, even if Mitra was wrong.
4. For a non-paying business: message the business through the deal room, then call. Note it on the business; a second case unlists it.

### E. Weekly (Friday)
1. Bill-book check at 3 shops. 2. Review the three numbers in `marketing.md`. 3. Reorder standees if needed. 4. In plan B only: run payouts, reconcile TDS.

## 7. The promo-video question

The founder's idea has pass holders make promotional videos for participating businesses. Operationally and ethically, this is the weakest part:

- Every creator panelist who mentioned it read it as **unpaid work they pay for**, including those for whom ₹499 is nothing.
- It has to be scheduled, checked and chased, and every video is a paid partnership needing an ASCI label.
- It undercuts creators' rate cards, which is the opposite of Mitra's promise (help creators get paid properly).

**Recommendation:** drop it as a condition of the pass. If businesses want videos, they post a brief and pay the creator's rate (already built).

## Verdict from operations

Plan B adds four recurring back-office jobs (attribution, holds, payouts, TDS), a registration that has to happen before any payout, a chasing job for promo videos, and two of the top risks. Plan C adds one field routine (the guarantee) and none of the money handling. **Run C; if a business incentive is wanted, make it in kind (setup, visibility, a free second creator), not cash.**
