# Mitra: vision and plan, final report (21 Oct 2026)

Inputs: the founder's roadmap (`roadmap.md`) and additions (`vision-additions.md`: affiliate earnings, an agent that finds nearby businesses online and matches them to creators, negotiation help, "AI CMO for every creator"). Reviewed by the three-member founder board on the vision alone (`board/`), and by a 20-creator simulated panel on the vision pitch (`consumer/`). Simulated, not customers; real creators decide.

## Verdict
**The vision is right and big enough; the plan needs re-ordering.** Board: **3 × FUND IF, 5.7/10** (Offers 6, Product 6, Monopoly 5), up from 3.7 when the same board also weighed today's traction. The idea passes; the plan's order and the trust design are what hold it back.

## What the board agrees is right
- The spine: **"Mitra tells you the 1–3 things worth doing this week, and shows you whether they worked."** Plan → content → measured result, fed back into memory.
- The durable asset: a **regional creator graph** built from first-party performance and real deal outcomes, owned one language at a time ("100 creators in one segment beat 1,000 scattered").
- The discipline: gates, "do not build yet", managed campaigns before marketplace code, approval before any external action.

## What the creator panel said (20 creators, same seed as Mitra's first panel)
- **0 of 20 would pay ₹299/month up front.** Most would start on the free plan. Reasons: trust 11, habit 4, need 3, price 2.
- **The vision created a new objection:** *audience trust*. 5 of 20 fear affiliate links and paid local deals make their viewers feel sold to ("my audience follows me because I feel like one of them"). 7 of 20 asked who talks to shops and brands in their name, and whether anything goes out without their OK.
- Still there: "YouTube Studio and ChatGPT do this for free", and "who made this app, what happens to my data".
- **What flips them, in order:** free proof on their own channel (the plan beats their usual views); **one real local deal that pays them before they pay Mitra**; every message to a business approved by them, with the fee shown first; a named team and a creator they follow vouching.
- Price range for Pro: about **₹80–₹300**, least resistance ~₹100, indifference ~₹150. ₹299 sits at the top edge. (Simulated: pick what to test, not proof.)

## The biggest risks
1. **Money arrives too late in the plan.** Creators are asked to pay for advice (Phases 1–3) long before Mitra puts money in their pocket (Phases 4–6), and money is what they want most. (Offers)
2. **The local-business side is a sales problem, not an agent problem.** Finding shops online is easy; getting a clinic to pay a creator is small-ticket, high-touch selling. Agent-drafted pitches at volume read as spam and cost the creator's reputation. Three-sided cold start in one city at once. (Monopoly, Offers)
3. **The money moment happens outside Mitra.** Shooting, publishing, the shop owner's reply on WhatsApp, haggling, UPI or cash: five hand-offs Mitra doesn't own, so no record, no fee, no measured outcome. (Product)
4. **Platform dependence.** YouTube owns the data and can put "your three actions this week" into Studio; its API rules (30-day refresh or delete; no sharing with third parties without consent) shape what the graph may keep and show brands. (Monopoly)
5. **Too many products for two founders.** Each done at 60%. (Product)

## The plan, revised
**One-line pitch** (Product): *"Mitra tells you what to make this week, proves it worked, and helps you get paid."* Retire "AI CMO" in front of creators: a job title, not a result, and many regional creators won't know the term. Test names in Hindi and Kannada.

**Phase 1 (now, separate branch): Plan + pack + proof + first money tools.** Merge roadmap Phases 1 and 2 at their smallest:
- "Today / This Week": 1–3 actions with the reason from the creator's own data, the ready-to-paste title/hook/script attached, and a measured before/after (grow the existing Retention Sprint and Progress page).
- Recommendation log with provenance and lifecycle (proposed → accepted → published → measured), honest "inconclusive" results.
- **Thin Earn layer pulled forward:** a media kit from their numbers, and "paste an offer you got" → Mitra flags missing terms and suggests a fair rate (the first negotiation helper).
- **Deal log from day one:** creators record deals and amounts (consented, private), so the graph starts now, not in Phase 7.
- Trust built in: approval before anything leaves Mitra, a visible team page, creator controls for data.

**In parallel, by hand (no code):**
- **Local-business pilot in one city + one language + one niche** (e.g. Kannada food creators in one city): the founders find businesses, the creator approves each pitch, close real paid deals, learn the price and the pitch. Set a target before starting.
- **One managed brand campaign** for a pool of 10–20 creators in that niche.
- **Affiliate test** with a handful of creators, opt-in, clearly disclosed, products the creator picks, never pushed (the audience-trust objection).

**Phase 2: Earn v1, inside Mitra.** Deal CRM, invoice + UPI payment link so the deal and the payment happen in Mitra (closes the money seam), affiliate offers matched to audience with "why this fits", audience-trust guardrails (disclosure labels; check India's ASCI influencer-disclosure rules), outcome-based pricing: free for growth, a fee only on income Mitra helped create, no fee until a first deal closes.

**Phase 3: Automate what the manual pilots proved.** The local-business matching agent (sources and terms checked; nothing sent without approval), managed campaigns with tooling, light repurposing. Shrink content generation and distribution: it's the most copied layer.

**Phase 4+: Marketplace, then Creator Business OS**, only once one language + niche + city has liquidity (briefs routinely get qualified creators; brands come back).

**Before building the graph (Phase 0, a written page):** what YouTube's rules let Mitra store past 30 days and show brands, the consent design, and a platform-risk plan (own the commercial record: deals, CRM, payments, relationships, which survive an API change).

## Gates
1. Phase 1 → 2: creators act on the weekly plan and a measured lift shows up for some (counting inconclusive honestly); at least a few creators use the offer checker / media kit with real offers.
2. Phase 2 → 3: manual local pilot closes paid deals against its target; at least one brand pays for a managed campaign; deals and payments are recorded in Mitra.
3. Phase 3 → 4: repeat deals in one segment without the founders' hands on every one.

## Name the beachhead (Monopoly's condition)
One language + one niche + one city, written down. Kannada fits what Mitra already has (beta language, the founder WhatsApp groups); pick the niche and city where local businesses visibly pay for marketing.

## What happens next
Phase 1 is built on its own branch, behind a feature flag, without touching the live beta's behaviour.
