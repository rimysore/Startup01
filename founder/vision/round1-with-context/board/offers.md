# Offers lens (new vision)
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## Verdict in two sentences
"An AI CMO that tells you the 1–3 things to do this week, writes the pack, and proves it worked against your own baseline" promises a far bigger outcome than "₹299 analytics", but today the likelihood term is zero: no real connected channel, no paying user, no documented result. The creator side is a hard market for money; the side with money (brands) is six phases and several gates away, so fund only the proof of Phases 1–2, not the ecosystem.

## What is strong
- **Dream outcome moves from "insight" to "more views and more income".** Selling the result, not the dashboard, is the right reframe; analytics alone was a commodity competing on price.
- **Effort drops.** 1–3 actions plus a ready Content Pack removes the work between "good idea" and "recorded video", the step generic tools leave to the creator.
- **Built-in proof machine.** Proposed → published → measured against baseline is exactly what the offer needs to build likelihood, and the Retention Sprint is already its first version.
- **Reachable buyers.** Creator WhatsApp groups and managers are cheap, concentrated channels; managers (~₹999) are the creator-side buyer with the most money.
- **Discipline.** Gates and "do not build yet" lists stop stack-bloat; transaction revenue "only when value is delivered" is a guarantee-shaped promise.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so no belief.** Every number is simulated. About half the "yes" buyers in the best panel only want a free WhatsApp check, not to connect a channel. If creators will not connect, the Creator Brain has nothing to learn from and the whole offer stalls.
2. **Buyers without money.** Small regional creators may value growth highly but pay little; 760 payers at ₹299 is needed just to break even with a salary. The AI CMO framing invites a higher price, but nothing shows they would pay it.
3. **The free substitute and the platform.** YouTube's free Ask Studio reads the channel and may cover Indian languages; API approval is still pending, and the 30-day data rule and the no-sharing-without-consent rule constrain the Creator Graph and brand-facing packages.

## Scale
Creator software alone looks like a small, price-sensitive business. The step-change is GMV: brand campaigns and affiliate take where the money sits with brands. That only works with density (the roadmap's "100 creators in one language/niche") and data you are allowed to use. The two-sided model is plausible but entirely unproven, and its economics (take rate, campaign size, brand demand for Kannada/Hindi creators) are open questions, not facts.

## Phase order: what you would change
- Collapse Phases 1–2 into one offer: the "Retention Sprint" becomes a named 4-week Growth Sprint (weekly actions + Content Pack + before/after card), with a guarantee such as continued free service until a measured lift.
- Pull a manual slice of Phase 4 (media kit + rate check for creators with any brand interest) forward as a bonus. Income is a stronger pull than views and it costs little to deliver by hand.
- Test the manager offer early; one manager is many channels.
- Move Phases 6–7 later only on evidence: before any marketplace code, sell one managed campaign by hand.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many of the 30 beta creators actually connected a channel, and how many acted on a recommendation?
- What is the guarantee, and can you afford it?
- Would a creator pay ₹999 for the AI CMO? Have you asked one directly with a real price?
- What does Mitra do that free Ask Studio does not, in the creator's own words?
- Has any brand or agency said it wants Kannada/Hindi creator pools, and at what budget?
- What is the plan if API verification is delayed or denied?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. By the end of the beta: at least 5 documented before/after results on real connected channels, measured against baseline.
2. At least 10 creators or managers pay a real price (not survey intent), with the price tested above ₹299 for the AI CMO offer.
3. Google OAuth verification and the Analytics amendment are approved, or there is a written fallback.
4. A named, affordable guarantee and one-line offer name (not "Video Doctor").
5. No brand-side build until one campaign is sold and delivered manually.
