# Offers lens (vision only)
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## Verdict in two sentences
The market is right and the plan is disciplined, but the offer is ordered back to front: the creator is asked to pay for advice (Phases 1-3) long before Mitra puts money in their pocket (Phases 4-6), and money is the outcome this buyer wants most. Lead with earning, name the offer after a result instead of a job title, and this becomes much easier to say yes to.

## What is strong
- **Market logic.** Regional-language creators have real pain (growth and income), they are easy to find (their channels are public), and the plan assumes the segment is growing. Whether it is growing, and how fast, is an open question the founder should back with a source.
- **Dream outcome is big and concrete:** "grow and earn." Both are things a creator can count.
- **Likelihood is designed in.** Recommendations tied to their own data, measured against their own baseline. That is the start of proof, which is where most offers fail.
- **Effort falls as the stack grows:** plan, then content pack, then repurposing, then negotiation. Each layer removes a problem the creator hits on the way to the outcome. That is a good stack.
- **"Fee only when value is delivered"** on deals is the right risk reversal for brands and creators.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The buyer with the most pain has the least money.** Small regional creators need help most and can pay least. A subscription sold on "better decisions" is a weak promise to someone with little money. Income arrives at Phase 4 or later, so the early offer has to justify itself on growth alone, and growth is slow and hard to prove.
2. **The local-business side may not pay.** An agent finding shops, clinics and coaching centres online does not mean they have marketing budgets, trust creators, or answer a cold pitch. If the creator approves 20 pitches and none convert, trust in Mitra dies on both sides. Unknown: what a local deal is worth, and how often one closes.
3. **Affiliate income may be too small to feel.** For small audiences, commissions may be tiny. If "Earn" shows a handful of rupees, it hurts the promise more than having no Earn tab at all. Open question: real commission per creator per month in the target niches.

## Scale
The software subscription grows with the number of creators but is limited by what small creators can pay. The take on deal and affiliate revenue grows with GMV and is the real upside, but it needs density by niche and language (the roadmap is right on this). Local deals are labour-heavy per rupee earned unless the matching and pitching truly run themselves. Open question: what a creator earns through Mitra at the median, not at the top.

## The plan: what you would change
- **Pull a thin Earn layer forward into Phase 1-2:** a media kit, rate guidance and offer analysis. That is cheap to deliver, valuable to the buyer, and it turns "advice" into money.
- **Pilot the local-business agent by hand in one city, one language, one niche** before automating it. Close real deals manually and learn the price and the pitch.
- **Price on outcome:** a free tier for growth, with the paid tier or the fee tied to income earned. Add a guarantee the numbers can afford (for example: no fee until a first deal closes).
- **Rename the offer.** "AI CMO" is a corporate title, not a result. Many regional creators may not know the term, and it promises vaguely. To a local business it reads as "marketing for my shop," which is a different product. Name the outcome instead: something like "your first paid deal," or "grow and get paid." Test the name with real creators, in their own language.
- **Give brands a separate offer** with its own promise: local, verified audiences, and pay only for performance.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which creators feel the pain and can pay today? At what audience size?
- What proof will you show a new creator in week one?
- How fast does a creator get a first result, and what result?
- What is a typical local deal worth, and who on the business side says yes?
- What do affiliate commissions actually pay in your first niche?
- Could you charge double if you tied the price to income earned?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Bring a basic Earn layer (media kit, rate guidance, offer analysis) into the first two phases.
2. Close at least a few manual local-business deals in one concentrated segment, with documented deal value, before building the agent.
3. Replace "AI CMO" with an outcome-led name tested with creators, and write a separate offer for businesses.
4. Ship a guarantee or outcome-based price the unit economics can carry.
