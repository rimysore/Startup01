# Monopoly lens (new vision)
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## Verdict in two sentences
The roadmap names the right monopoly shape: a dense language-plus-niche network where brands come because the creators are there and creators come because the brands are. Today none of that exists. The moat only appears in phase 7, the wedge is copyable and sits on top of YouTube, and the plan has no secret beyond "regional-native."

## What is strong
- **It starts with a small market.** "100 high-quality creators in one segment beat 1,000 scattered" is the right instinct. Kannada is a small enough market to own if anyone tried.
- **The managed service comes before the marketplace.** Phase 6 builds the demand side by hand before writing marketplace code. That is how a two-sided network solves cold start.
- **The data asset is real if it gets built.** Outcome-linked data (recommendation, then publication, then measured delta, then campaign result) is harder to copy than a feature. Follower-count directories do not have it.
- **The gates are honest.** Each phase must prove itself before the next starts.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **YouTube owns the ground and the rules.** Ask Studio is free, reads the channel and may support Indian languages, and it can absorb phases 1 to 3. The API policy (refresh or delete authorized data after 30 days, no selling or sharing with third parties without consent) works directly against a persistent Creator Brain and a graph shown to brands. OAuth verification is still pending and the Analytics amendment has not been submitted. The moat would be built on data Mitra may not be allowed to keep or share.
2. **Cold start with no distribution.** There are 0 payers and no channel has been tested live. Recruiting has under ₹25K and one part-time founder in India. The most enthusiastic simulated segment mostly commits only to a free WhatsApp check. A two-sided network needs density on the creator side before any brand cares, and nothing here shows how 100 creators in one niche arrive or what each one costs.
3. **Copycats at both ends.** VidUp and vidIQ-style tools can add Kannada titles and hooks in a quarter, since the wedge is incremental and not 10x better. Indian influencer agencies and platforms already have brand relationships and could add regional creator pools faster than Mitra can add brands. Their regional coverage has not been researched. That is a blind spot, not a comfort.

## Scale
The vision is large: software plus a take on creator commerce. But each phase is a crowded category of its own (analytics, AI writing, scheduling, media kits, affiliate, influencer marketing), and Mitra would enter every one without a lead. Scale only matters if one niche locks in first. Today it does not.

## Phase order: what you would change
- **Pull a manual version of phase 6 forward.** Run one brand campaign for 10 to 20 Kannada creators in a single niche as soon as a handful of channels are connected. Brand money is the strongest pull for creators and the only real test of the graph thesis.
- **Fold phases 2, 3 and 8 together and shrink them.** They are features YouTube and the incumbents will ship anyway.
- **Treat data rights as phase 0.** Before designing the Creator Graph, settle what can legally be stored past 30 days and shown to brands with creator consent.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single niche plus language will you own first, and how many creators is "owning" it?
- What do you know about regional creators or brands that YouTube, vidIQ and the Indian agencies do not?
- Under the 30-day and no-sharing rules, what part of the graph survives?
- How do the first 100 creators in that niche arrive, and at what cost each?
- Who is the first brand that would pay for a Kannada creator pool, and have you spoken to them?
- If Ask Studio does all of phase 1 for free in Kannada next year, what is left?
