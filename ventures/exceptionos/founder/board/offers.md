# Offers lens (round 2)
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What changed since round 1
Better: the offer now goes after the two places offers usually fail. Likelihood: proof on the buyer's own data replaces a promise, and that is the thing 18 of 20 simulated buyers said would change their mind. Effort: read-only, offline, sanitized exports, no credentials, so the IT/security gate gets much smaller. The one-sentence pitch is clear and the kill rule is honest.
Worse: the price signal is gone. Zero of 20 would sign at $2,000/month, and now there is no price at all, so the business has no evidence anyone will pay. The market also got smaller: Epicor Kinetic machinery shops are some unknown slice of the 13,002 firms.

Round-1 conditions:
- Narrow plus a one-sentence pitch: **met**.
- Map incumbent coverage: **partly met**. Competitors are mapped, but what Epicor's own MRP and shortage reports already show is still unknown, and that is the competitor that matters most.
- 3 design partners with a measured baseline, delivered by hand: **not met**. It is a plan, with 0 interviews reported.
- 1 paid pilot: **not met**. It was removed rather than earned.
- Written offer: **partly met**. The free audit is written. The paid continuation has no scope, price or guarantee.
- Alert accuracy bar: **not met**. There is a killer metric but no precision threshold.
- Channel to customer 100 and a named team: **not met**.
- OS off the roadmap: **met**.

## What is strong
- The audit is close to a good front-end offer: low effort, quick to say yes to, and it ends in a question ("which did your team already know?") that measures value instead of claiming it.
- The positioning ("tells you what the change will break") sells an outcome, not a feature, and sits next to SourceDay and Didero instead of fighting them on price.
- The killer metric, missed high-impact exceptions, is countable and buyer-facing.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Epicor already does it well enough.** If time-phase and shortage reports plus a good planner catch these changes, the dream outcome drops to roughly zero. The founder has written this down as the kill rule, but nobody has tested it.
2. **The free audit ends at "thanks."** A free report with no next step priced, no guarantee and no urgency produces gratitude, not revenue. One missed exception may not justify a recurring purchase unless it is tied to dollars: a line stoppage, an expedite or a late customer order.
3. **Still too much effort for a small team.** Pulling clean open-PO, work-order, BOM, inventory and date-change history out of Kinetic is real work for a 50–500 person shop. If the date-change history is not there, the core signal may not be there either.

## Scale
Delivery is done by hand in Python and CSVs, so it scales only as fast as the founder's hours. That is fine for 3 audits and is not a business. The stack that could scale is ongoing monitoring of the same export, a weekly ranked list and a dollar figure per caught exception. The market is the unknown count of Epicor Kinetic users among U.S. machinery makers, and whether it is growing is not known. Doubling the price is not a question you can ask without a price.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many of the 5 interviews have happened, and what did they say?
- On one real dataset, what would Kinetic's shortage reports have flagged anyway?
- Does the typical shop keep supplier date-change history at all?
- What does a caught exception cost them in dollars: an expedite, overtime or a late shipment?
- What is the paid offer, at what price, and what is the guarantee?
- How many hours does one audit take you?
- Who is on the team, and how is it funded?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Three real audits are done, with the precision of the ranked list and the number of missed exceptions reported against what Kinetic already shows.
2. At least one missed exception is valued in dollars by the buyer, not by us.
3. The paid continuation is written down, with scope, a price a real buyer has heard, and a guarantee you can afford (for example, no fee for a month with no high-impact catch).
4. One buyer pays, at any price.
5. A named team and an audit time of under X hours, with X stated.
