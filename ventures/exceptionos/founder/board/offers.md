# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market passes three of the four tests.** The pain is real: buyers chase changed dates, short shipments and missing acknowledgements by hand. Manufacturers at $30M–$300M have budgets. A narrow profile (named ERPs, verticals, 3–30 buyers) makes them easy to find. Whether the market is growing is unknown.
- **The dream outcome is concrete.** "No line stops and no customer order slips because nobody caught a supplier change" ties to revenue, not clerical time. Ranking by downstream impact rather than PO value is the right outcome to sell.
- **Delivering by hand first** through concierge design partners is the cheapest way to prove the result.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood: there is no proof.** No customers, pilots or case study. A buyer has to believe a newcomer will connect to their ERP, inbox and spreadsheets and rank exceptions better than their own planner does.
2. **Effort for the buyer.** To start, they must grant email and ERP access, get through a security review and keep a weekly champion for 4–8 weeks before any result. If onboarding feels like an ERP project, the deal dies.
3. **There is no offer, so it risks becoming a price play.** Price, package, guarantee and name are all undecided. If ERP modules, portals or procurement suites already flag mismatches (the brief says their coverage is unknown), the pitch falls back to "a cheaper add-on", which makes it a commodity.

## Scale
Size = qualifying U.S. manufacturers × what each pays a year × the ERPs it supports. To grow large, four things must hold: (a) the same 1–2 exceptions recur across companies, so one product fits many; (b) setup takes days and repeats across ERPs; (c) the price is anchored to prevented downtime or late orders, not to seats; (d) the history it builds up keeps customers and pulls them into the wider "Exception OS". Company count, contract value and retention: no figures exist, so all are open questions. If (a), (b) or (c) fails, it stays a niche that leans on consulting.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What did one missed exception cost a prospect last quarter, and who gave you that number?
- What do their ERP and supplier portal already catch, and what slips through?
- What is the least data access that gets a first result, and how many days does it take?
- What will you guarantee, e.g. "impact-ranked exceptions in 14 days or the pilot is free"?
- Could you double the price and still be the obvious choice? What would the stack need for that?
- Who is on the team?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Three design partners that each meet 5 of the 7 criteria, with a measured baseline and a result delivered by hand.
2. One paid pilot priced on measured impact, not a free trial.
3. A written offer with a name, a price, the stack (detection, impact ranking, drafted follow-ups, resolution tracking), a promised time to first result and an affordable guarantee.
4. A one-page map of incumbent coverage that proves the gap is real.
