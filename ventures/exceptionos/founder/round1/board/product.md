# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is a real "one thing" in the brief: **rank supplier delivery exceptions by what they break downstream, not by PO value.** It is a sharp idea of what the product is for.
- The "explicitly NOT" list (no chatbot, no summarizer, no ERP replacement) shows the founder can say no.
- Delivering value by hand to design partners first is the right way to find the experience before building it.
- The hard part is in the right place: deciding impact and driving resolution, not extraction.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Scope sprawl.** Ingest four formats, detect six exception types, rank, gather evidence, draft actions, track to closure, across nine ERPs and five verticals, with an "OS" vision on top. That is five products, and none of them will get enough craft to earn trust. The pitch needs a paragraph, so the product is not clear yet.
2. **Seams sit exactly where trust is earned.** The chain runs email to extraction, to a PO match, to BOM and work-order links, to a ranking, to a drafted message, to approval. One wrong "this stops Line 3 Thursday" alert, or one missed exception, and the buyer goes back to Outlook.
3. **No moment has been described.** Nobody has written the buyer's first five minutes. Without it, the build starts from the data pipes, which is backwards.

## Scale
If it works, it grows from a buyer's triage tool into the place every operational exception gets handled: supplier, inventory, work order, customer order. That needs four things to be true:
- One exception type is solved so well that buyers stop checking the inbox for it.
- The ranking is trusted without re-checking.
- Each new ERP connection is repeatable rather than custom work.
- The context it builds up makes each month more valuable than the last.

How big the market is and how many target firms exist are open questions. The brief gives no figures.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What does it do, in one sentence with no commas?
- Which single exception type would you keep? Is it changed promise dates on parts that feed open work orders?
- What does the buyer see on screen in their first five minutes on Monday?
- What share of targets have BOM and work-order data clean enough to rank impact? What happens to the experience when the data is not clean?
- How many wrong or missed alerts would a buyer tolerate? Nobody has this number yet.
- Team, funding and price are all still unstated.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Cut to one exception type, one ERP, one vertical and one persona, and get the pitch to one sentence.
2. Storyboard the buyer's daily experience and test it by hand with 3 design partners before building any pipeline.
3. Show evidence that the ranking changed what a buyer did, measured against that partner's baseline.
4. Set an accuracy bar for alerts and design what the buyer sees when the data is uncertain.
5. Keep the "OS" off the roadmap until 3 paid pilots exist.
