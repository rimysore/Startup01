# Discovery interview guide: capital planning & facilities

Goal: find out **where data and reporting time goes**, what leaders want to know but can't easily see, and what it costs. We're learning, not pitching. Don't mention a product until the last 5 minutes, and only to outside interviewees.

## What we know so far (from our own office)
- Systems: **AiM** (work orders, assets, maintenance), **e-Builder** (capital projects, budgets, schedules, change orders), **Excel** (everything in between).
- Most painful: **board reports** and **forecasting**.
- The VP's top ask: **"data, any data, all data about our work: how efficient are we?"**

**Working hypothesis (to test):** the pain is not missing data. It's that AiM, e-Builder and Excel don't talk to each other, so every leadership question turns into a manual spreadsheet project. An agent that joins them and produces KPIs, forecasts and board-ready narrative automatically would save weeks per cycle.

---

## Ground rules (from *The Mom Test*)
1. Ask about **the past and specifics** ("the last time…"), not the future ("would you use…").
2. Ask **how much it cost**: hours, consultants, overtime, mistakes.
3. Talk less than 30% of the time. Silence is fine.
4. Ask for **artifacts**: "Can you show me the last version of that report?" (Outside institutions only, and only if public or they offer.)
5. End every interview with: **"Who else should I talk to?"**

## ⚠️ Interviewing your own leadership
- Read IU's outside-activity, conflict-of-interest and IP policies **before** these conversations. Consider telling your manager about the side project. Being open protects you, and many leaders will want to help.
- Conversations inside IU are excellent **practice and learning**, but they're one customer and they know you, so they'll be kind. **Real validation must come from at least 15 people at other institutions.**
- Take no IU files, exports or screenshots home. Write notes in your own words about *types* of problems, not IU numbers.

## Question bank by role

### 1. VP / Associate VP (the economic buyer)
1. When you say "how efficient are we," what exactly do you want to see? Which 3 numbers would you look at every Monday?
2. Tell me about the last time you needed a number and couldn't get it quickly. What happened? How long did it take?
3. Who asks *you* for data (president, CFO, board, state, accreditors)? What do they ask for most?
4. How do you compare us to peer institutions today? Do you trust those comparisons?
5. What decision did you make last year that you wish had better data behind it?
6. What has the division bought in the last 2–3 years (software, consultants, assessments)? Who approved it, and how long did purchasing take?
7. If reporting took a tenth of the time, what would your team do with the hours?

### 2. Director of capital planning / budget (the daily user and champion)
1. Walk me through the **last board report**, step by step: where does each number come from?
2. Which parts are copy-paste from AiM or e-Builder exports? Which parts are judgment and writing?
3. How many people and hours go into one board cycle? How many board cycles per year?
4. How do you **forecast**: project spend, cash flow, deferred maintenance growth, future capital needs? In what tool? How accurate was last year's forecast?
5. When was the last condition assessment? Who did it, what did it cost, and how current is it now?
6. Where do numbers disagree between systems (AiM vs. e-Builder vs. the finance system)? How do you reconcile them?
7. What report or analysis do you *wish* you could produce but never have time for?

### 3. Project managers (e-Builder users)
1. How much of your week goes to status updates and reporting rather than managing projects?
2. Tell me about the last time a project's budget or schedule surprised leadership. How was it discovered?
3. How are change orders, contingency and cash flow tracked and rolled up?

### 4. Maintenance / work control (AiM users)
1. Which AiM reports do you run regularly? Who reads them?
2. How do you measure backlog, response time, preventive vs. corrective maintenance? Is the data trustworthy (closed-out correctly, coded consistently)?
3. Do repeat work orders on the same equipment ever feed into capital planning? How?

### 5. Finance / budget office (the second buyer or blocker)
1. How do facilities numbers get to you, and how often do they need fixing?
2. What do state reports or bond disclosures require from facilities data?

### Closing (outside institutions only)
- "If something did [their top pain] for you, who would pay for it and from what budget?"
- "Would you be open to looking at an early version built on public data in a few weeks?"
- "Who else should I talk to?"

## Notes template (one per interview)
```
Date / role / institution type (R1, regional public, small private, K-12…)
Systems used:
Top 3 pains (their words, quoted):
Last concrete example + time/cost:
Who pays / who approves / buying process:
Current workarounds & spend (consultants, staff hours, tools):
Strength of pain 1–5:   Would pilot? Y/N/maybe
Referrals:
```

## What counts as a signal
- 🟢 Strong: "We spent $X on consultants for this," "two people lose 3 weeks every quarter," "can I see it now?", unprompted referrals, sharing a sample report.
- 🟡 Weak: "That's interesting," "we'd probably use that," compliments.
- 🔴 Kill signal: pain exists but nobody owns a budget for it, or "AiM's new module already does that and we're happy."
