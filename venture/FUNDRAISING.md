# Getting support: accelerators, cohorts and investors

Written 2026-10-09. Dates and programs change often. **Check each program's own site before applying.**

## The honest starting point
Investors at this stage back **people + insight + early proof**. Today we have strong insight and founder-market fit (you do this job, and leadership praised your reports), a working prototype and a public-data demo.
What they'll worry about, and how we fix each before asking for money:

| Concern | Fix before raising |
|---|---|
| **Employer IP and conflict of interest** | Disclose to IU and get written clarity. Investors will ask; unclear IP blocks a deal. |
| **No technical cofounder** | Recruit one. This is the single biggest gap for accelerators like YC. |
| **No customer proof yet** | 15–20 interviews, then 3 design partners, ideally 1–2 paying pilots or signed letters of intent |
| **Market looks smallish (~$117M higher ed)** | Show the expansion path and price evidence from interviews |

**Order of operations:** support before money. Programs that give mentors, intros and credibility come first. Investor money comes after design partners.

## Track 1: start inside IU (it solves the IP question too)
Talking to IU's startup channels is both the conflict-of-interest step and a source of support.
- **IU Ventures** supports IU students, faculty, **staff** and alumni. Programs listed publicly include the IU Angel Network, IU Philanthropic Venture Fund, Innovate Indiana Fund, Shoebox Fund, the Founders & Funders Network, and The Quarry (federal SBIR/STTR grant help, plus "Quarry AI" for AI/software projects). The university-wide **IU Innovates** initiative recently expanded startup support.
- **Ask them:** "I'm an IU staff member with a software idea in facilities analytics. What's the right disclosure path, and which programs fit?"
- **Benefit:** a clean IP answer, local angels, mentors, and possibly IU as a sanctioned first customer.

## Track 2: Indiana ecosystem
- **Elevate Ventures** (Indiana's state-backed early-stage investor). Its Nexus pitch competitions have historically awarded pre-seed ($20k–40k) and seed ($80k–100k) to Indiana-based startups; university engagement is preferred. *2026 cycle details not confirmed; check their site.*
- **gener8tor gBETA Indiana:** free, equity-free, cohorts of up to 5 companies. The 2026 cycles have closed; the next is likely **winter 2027** *(estimate)*.
- **TechPoint** (Indiana tech community): events, mentors and investor introductions.

## Track 3: national accelerators
| Program | What it gives | Fit and timing |

| **Y Combinator** | $500k standard deal, network, Demo Day | Now 4 batches a year. **Winter 2027 deadline reported as early November 2026** (unconfirmed; check ycombinator.com/apply). ## Track 4: investors who understand this market
Approach these **after** 3 design partners. Check each fund's current stage and focus first.
- **Built environment / proptech:** MetaProp (NYC; pre-seed and seed lead), Shadow Ventures (seed; proptech and construction tech), Nine Four Ventures (Chicago; proptech, stage focus varies by source), Zacua Ventures (built-environment, more construction and climate).
- **Education:** education-focused funds (e.g. Reach Capital, Owl Ventures, Learn Capital). Most back learning products, so pitch the "institutional operations" angle and check fit first.
- **Govtech:** the CivStart accelerator is winding down, so look for govtech investors when we expand into K-12 and cities.
- **Angels (often the best first check):** former facilities VPs, and ex-executives from Gordian, AssetWorks, Accruent, e-Builder or Brightly. They understand the problem and open doors. Find them through APPA and LinkedIn.

## Track 5: non-dilutive money
- Pitch competitions (IU/Kelley, Elevate, regional).
- **SBIR/STTR** federal grants, with help from IU Ventures' Quarry. The program's authorization status has been in flux, so confirm it's accepting applications.
- Cloud and AI credits (AWS, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic startup programs).

## How to approach (the mechanics)
1. **Warm intros beat cold email.** Ask every mentor, interviewee and IU Ventures contact: "Who's one investor or program I should talk to?"
2. **Two versions of the deck:** the 17-slide deck for presenting, and a 10-slide "send" version that reads on its own.
3. **Send a monthly update** to everyone who's helped or shown interest: interviews done, what you learned, design partners, the ask. Investors invest in trajectory; updates show it.
4. **Ask for advice first, money later.** "Could I get 20 minutes of feedback on the idea and our plan?" turns into "When you raise, let me know."
5. **Lead with the live demo** (Metric Studio, UMass edition): the 54-vs-55 moment, then personal dashboards.

## Short intro email (to a program manager or investor)

**Subject:** Facilities analytics for universities: 20 minutes of feedback?

```
Hi [Name],

I'm [your name]. I work in capital planning and facilities at a large public university, and I'm building an AI analyst that turns work-order, project and capital-plan data into board-ready numbers people can trust. Every number is calculated from a definition you can read, not guessed by AI.

I have a working prototype running on a public university capital plan: [link]. I'm interviewing facilities leaders at other campuses now.

Would you have 20 minutes for feedback on the idea and on whether [program/fund] is a fit at this stage?

Thanks,
[Your name] · [LinkedIn]
```
