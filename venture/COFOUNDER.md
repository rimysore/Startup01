# Finding a technical cofounder

Written 2026-10-09. This is the most important gap in the company; accelerators and investors will ask about it first.

## Who we're looking for
- **Must have:** full-stack engineer who has shipped a data-heavy web product end to end (backend, database, front end), and can work with LLM APIs.
- **Strong plus:** data pipelines/ETL, analytics or BI tools, enterprise SaaS security basics (SSO, roles, audit logs), experience selling to or working in universities, government or facilities.
- **Mindset:** cares about correctness (our product's promise is numbers you can trust), likes talking to customers, wants vertical B2B rather than a consumer app.
- **Commitment:** can go full-time once we have design partners or funding; until then, nights and weekends on a time-boxed trial.

## Where to look (in order)
1. **YC Co-Founder Matching** (free, via Startup School). Create a profile, wait for approval (up to about a week), then send personalized invites (the cap has been 15 a week). Profile text is below.
2. **Your own network:** engineers you've worked with on data or reporting tools; classmates; people who built internal dashboards at other campuses.
3. **University engineering and CS communities:** recent grads and grad students from computer science and data science programs, startup clubs, and hackathons. Check your employer's policy before recruiting IU students or staff, and keep all work outside IU.
4. **Indiana tech community:** TechPoint events, Indianapolis and Bloomington tech meetups, gener8tor/Elevate events.
5. **People who build adjacent products:** engineers at facilities-software, construction-tech or BI companies who are frustrated by building systems of record and want to build the analysis layer.
6. **Online:** LinkedIn (search "data engineer" + "higher education" or "facilities"), Indie Hackers, founder Slack and Discord communities.


## How to evaluate (don't skip this)
1. **First call (30 min):** motivation, what they've built, why vertical B2B, availability, location, money situation.
2. **Second call (60 min):** walk through the prototype together, and ask how they'd build the real version: data model, connectors, security.
3. **Trial project (2–4 weeks, part-time):** a scoped real task, e.g. "load a CSV work-order export into a proper database and serve three certified metrics through an API". You do customer interviews in parallel and share notes. Judge speed, quality, communication and how you resolve disagreements.
4. **Reference checks:** talk to 1–2 people they've worked with.
5. **Hard conversations before committing:** roles and decision rights, equity split, vesting, time commitment, what happens if one person leaves, salary expectations once funded. YC's co-founder resources include an agreement template.

## Equity and legal basics
- Standard: **4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff** for both founders, including you.
- Equal or near-equal splits are common for true cofounders who join early. Unequal splits are fine if the reasons are clear and written down.
- Form the company and sign IP assignment agreements **after** your employer conversation is resolved, so the company's IP is clean from day one.

## Red flags
- Wants to join only if paid now, or only "advises" with no commitment
- Has never finished and shipped anything end to end
- Dismisses customer interviews or the correctness requirement
- Pushes to build lots of features before talking to users
- Avoids the equity, vesting or exit conversation

## Weekly goal until found
- 10 personalized invites or messages per week (YC platform plus network)
- 3 first calls per week
- Start one trial project within 4 weeks
