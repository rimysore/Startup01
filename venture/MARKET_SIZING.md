## Method: bottom-up
Market = (number of institutions that would buy) × (what they'd pay per year).

## 3. "Where does the money come from?" (the stronger argument)
We don't need a new budget line. We can **replace money already being spent**:
- **Condition assessment consultants:** campuses pay outside firms every 3–5 years. A large campus can spend from hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars per cycle *(verify actual per-square-foot rates in interviews)*.
- **Analyst and staff time:** if 2–3 people each lose ~25% of their year to reporting and reconciliation, that's ~$75–150k/yr of salary per institution *(assumption)*.
- **Bad decisions:** one capital project misprioritized, or one emergency failure that should have been predicted, costs far more than the software.

**Pricing anchor to test:** less than one analyst's salary, and well below one condition assessment cycle.

## 4. What this means
- **Higher ed alone (~$100M/yr)** can support a great company in the $10–30M revenue range. That's very attractive for a bootstrapped or lightly funded business.
- **For venture capital**, the pitch needs the expansion story: higher ed → K-12 → public sector → healthcare facilities. The product core (joining work order + project + finance data into KPIs, forecasts and reports) is the same across all of them.
- Comparable software companies in this space (Gordian, AssetWorks, Brightly, Accruent, e-Builder, now part of Trimble) show buyers do pay for facilities and capital software. Our angle is that they **store** data and we **do the analysis**.
